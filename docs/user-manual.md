# Ether User Manual — version 4.6.71

This manual follows Ether's own menus. Each chapter says what a part of Ether is for, where to find it, and how to use
it step by step. The **Plan** line under a chapter says which plans include it: **Local**, **Live 128**, **Live 320** or
**Network** (see *Your plan* in Chapter 1).

---

# 1. Getting started

## What it's for

This chapter takes you from a new computer to the main screen. Everything in Ether hangs off your Ether account: your account carries your plan, your plan carries your stations, and your stations carry their logs and music libraries. The computer is only a terminal. Sign in on any computer and your stations come with you. This chapter covers installing Ether, signing in, setting up or picking your stations, choosing your profile, updating Ether, and signing out.

## Where to find it

- **Install:** run the Ether installer (**Ether Setup** *version* **.exe** on Windows, a **.dmg** on Mac).
- **Sign in:** the first screen every time you open Ether.
- **Profiles:** the **Select your profile** screen after sign-in. Manage them in **File → Settings → Station → Users & Security**.
- **Switch station:** the station button at the bottom-left of the main screen.
- **Updates:** the update card at the bottom-right, or **Help → Check for Updates**.
- **Sign out:** **File → Sign Out**.

**Plan:** Local (every plan). New accounts start with a 15-day trial; no card is needed. Pulling stations or music down from the cloud needs Live 128, Live 320 or Network (see each step below).

## 1. Install Ether

**On Windows**

1. Double-click **Ether Setup** *version* **.exe**.
2. The installer asks where to install. Keep the suggested folder or choose your own.
3. Finish the installer. It puts an **Ether** shortcut on the desktop and in the Start menu.

**On a Mac**

1. Open the Ether **.dmg** file.
2. Drag Ether into your Applications folder.

## 2. Sign in (every time you open Ether)

When you open Ether you see a short start-up screen, then the sign-in screen. **The sign-in screen always comes first.** Nothing else in Ether opens until an account is signed in. Once you are in, you stay in for as long as Ether runs; the sign-in renews itself in the background.

1. Open Ether.
2. Make sure **Sign in** is selected at the top.
3. Type your **Email address** and **Password**.
4. Leave **Remember me** ticked to keep this computer signed in while Ether runs. **File → Sign Out** ends it.
5. Press **Sign in** (or press Enter in the password box).

**Forgot password?** under the button opens the password reset page in your web browser.

If something goes wrong, the screen says why, in plain words. For example:

- **Email or password is incorrect.**
- **Couldn't reach the server to load your stations.** Check your internet connection and try again.
- **This account's devices are full.** Free up a device, then sign in again.

### New to Ether? Create an account

1. On the sign-in screen, choose **Sign up**.
2. Type **Your name (optional)**, your **Email address**, and a password of 8 or more characters.
3. Optional: type a handle for your public station page (the box that starts **listen.ether-technologies.com/@**). The screen tells you if it is **Available**, **Already taken**, or not allowed. You can also set it later.
4. Press **Create account & continue**. Your trial starts right away.

## 3. Your stations on this computer

What happens next depends on what your account already has.

### Your account has no stations yet

Ether asks you to create one.

1. **Name your new station.** Type the **Station name**. **Nickname**, **Frequency** and **Call letters** are optional.
2. Press **Create station**.
3. **How do you want to broadcast?** Pick **Solo**, **Standard** or **Live Radio**, then **Continue**. You can change this later in Settings.
4. **What are you using Ether for?** Pick the closest match (Radio Station, Venue / Attraction, Retail / Business, House of Worship, Podcast / YouTube), then **Continue**.
5. **Name your station.** Check the **Station name** and add a **Tagline** if you like. This shows in the header and on your Now Playing screen. Press **Continue**.
6. **Add your music.** Choose one:
   - **From this computer** — pick a folder. Ether matches files by name and links the ones it recognises.
   - **From the cloud** — downloads your cloud music library. **Plan:** Live 128, Live 320 or Network.
   - **Skip for now** — add music later through the Library.

### Your account already has stations

You see **Sync your stations to this computer**, with a card for each of your stations. All of them are ticked to start with.

1. Tick the stations this computer should have. **Select all** / **Clear all** appear when you have more than one.
2. If someone has given you access to their stations, they appear under **AVAILABLE TO YOU**. Tick any you want on this computer. **Plan:** Live 128, Live 320 or Network (account members).
3. Press **Sync** *(number)* **selected**.
4. **Where should we keep your music?** Keep **Default app folder** or press **Choose folder…**, then press **Install here**.
5. Ether shows **Getting your station ready on this computer** with a percentage. Your stations, profiles, colour theme, shows and rotation schedules come down from the cloud, and the music library downloads once for all of them. When it finishes, Ether restarts into your stations.

If setup does not finish, the screen says **Setup didn't finish** and offers **Retry**.

Other choices on this screen:
- **+ Create new station** — make a new station instead (the steps in the section above).
- **Skip** — go on without pulling anything down.

**Plan:** pulling your stations from the cloud uses Cloud backup and the Cloud music library: Live 128, Live 320 or Network.

If the stations are already on this computer (for example after reinstalling Ether), Ether opens them straight away without downloading anything.

## 4. Choose your profile

After your account is signed in, you choose who is working. A **profile** is a person who uses this copy of Ether, such as "Admin" or "Jeff". Profiles have nothing to do with your account. They live on this computer.

### The first time

There are no profiles yet, so Ether asks you to make one.

1. Under **Set up your profile**, type a **Profile title** (for example your name). It starts as "Admin".
2. Type a 4-digit **PIN (4 digits)**, then type it again in **Confirm PIN**.
3. Press **Create profile & continue**.

### Every time after that

1. Under **Select your profile**, click your name. Each card shows the profile's role and **PIN required** or **No PIN**.
2. If the profile has a PIN, type it on the number pad or on your keyboard. Ether checks it as soon as you type the fourth digit.
3. A wrong PIN shows **Incorrect PIN** and clears the dots. **← Back** (or Esc) returns to the list.

A profile with no PIN opens as soon as you click it.

### Roles

Each profile has a role: **Administrator**, **Music Director** or **On-Air Jock**. Today the role is a label. It does not limit what the profile can see or do.

### Start your shift

After the profile, you see the **On Shift** screen: who is on shift, what is on air now and coming up, station health, and **Personal Notes** (type a note; it saves itself). Press **Start My Shift →** to open the main screen.

### Change profile while Ether is running

Click the **person** button in the top bar, next to **AUTO**. You go back to **Select your profile**. Your account stays signed in.

### Add or change profiles

Open **File → Settings → Station → Users & Security**.

- **+ Add User** — type a **Name**, pick a role and a colour, and an optional 4-digit PIN. Press **Create User**.
- **Edit** — change the name, role or colour, then **Save**.
- **Set PIN** / **Change PIN** — type a new 4-digit PIN twice and press **Save PIN**. Leave both boxes empty and press **Save PIN** to take the PIN away.

## 5. Your stations after sign-in

The station button at the bottom-left of the main screen shows the station you are working on (its call sign, or the start of its name) with a green dot.

1. Click it to see your stations. The one you are in has a tick.
2. Click another station to switch to it. A note says **Switched to** *(name)*.
3. **New Station…** opens a form: **Station Name** (required), **Callsign**, and its **ICECAST SETTINGS** (**Server URL**, **Mount**, **Password**, **Bitrate**, **Format**). Press **Create Station**.
4. **Manage Stations…** opens the Station Manager.

Stations other accounts have shared with you are listed under **ACCESSIBLE VIA YOUR ACCOUNT**. They are view-only here.

**Plan:** Local (every plan).

## 6. Updating Ether

Ether checks for a new version about 10 seconds after it starts, and every 30 minutes while it runs.

1. When there is a new version, a card appears at the bottom-right: **Ether** *(version)* **is ready**, with notes about the release.
2. Press **Update Now**. The card shows **Downloading update…** and its progress.
3. When it says **Update downloaded**, press **Restart & Update**.
4. **Then close Ether completely and open it again.** The audio engine runs separately from the window and does not restart with an update. To close completely, use **File → Quit Ether**, or close the window and choose **Stop & Quit Ether**.

**Later** hides the card for now. A downloaded update also installs the next time Ether quits.

To check yourself, use **Help → Check for Updates**. If a newer version exists, the same card appears. If you are up to date, nothing appears.

If the audio engine is still the old version, a yellow bar across the top says so: **The audio engine is running an older build … fully close and reopen Ether.** It stays until you do.

## 7. Signing out

1. Choose **File → Sign Out**.
2. Ether asks **Sign out?** — "Ether will close completely, including the audio engine. Reopen it to sign in."
3. Press **Sign Out**.

Ether closes completely. Nothing is deleted. Your stations, library and settings are kept for this account, and signing back in opens them exactly as you left them. To use a different account on this computer, sign out, open Ether again and sign in as the other account. Each account keeps its own stations; neither can see the other's.

## 8. Your plan

Your plan comes from your Ether account. You never type a license key: signing in with your account's email and
password is all Ether needs. To see your plan, open **☰ → Plans & Subscription** (or press the plan name in the top
bar).

| Plan | What it's for |
|---|---|
| **Local** | The full desktop app on every computer, streaming to your own Icecast server. No cloud features. |
| **Live 128** | One hosted station at 128 kbps, plus every cloud feature: web dashboard, remote control, sync between computers, cloud backup and the cloud music library. |
| **Live 320** | The same as Live 128, at up to 320 kbps. |
| **Network** | Every feature, for a group of stations. |

Prices are shown in **Plans & Subscription**, monthly or yearly (two months free on a yearly plan). New accounts start
with a 15-day free trial; no card is needed. To change plans, press **Choose** on a plan there, or go to your account
page in a web browser.

## 9. Billing notices

If something needs your attention about your plan, a small notice appears at the top right of Ether, for example:

- your free trial ends soon, or has ended;
- a payment failed;
- your card has expired or is about to.

Each notice says what happened and has a button to fix it (it opens your account page). **Later** hides it.

- The same notice shows **at most once a day**. A different, more serious notice shows right away.
- **A billing notice never stops playout.** Your station keeps playing; only the cloud features pause if a trial or
  plan has ended.
- After you fix it (for example, update your card), the notice goes away on its own.

## Good to know

- **No account, no Ether.** Until an account is signed in, the sign-in screen is the only screen.
- **Signing out stops the audio.** A signed-out account does not broadcast. Take the station off air before you sign out.
- **One exception to "sign in first":** if the computer restarts while a station is streaming live, Ether comes back on air by itself without waiting for a sign-in.
- **Closing the window is not quitting.** Closing the main window asks what to do: **Keep Playing in Tray** (audio keeps going; find Ether in the system tray), **Stop & Quit Ether**, or **Cancel**. If the tray icon could not be made, the choices are only **Stop & Quit Ether** and **Cancel**.
- **Solo, Standard and Live Radio** is stored for the station but does not change which decks the board shows. You set the board up yourself (see *Decks and the board*).

# 2. The main screen

## What it's for

The main screen is where you run the station. It shows what is playing and what is next, the board with its faders, and the controls you need on air. Everything else opens beside it in its own window, so the live view stays on screen. This chapter is a map: what each part of the screen is, and where every menu entry and button goes.

## Where to find it

It opens when you press **Start My Shift →** after choosing your profile. From anywhere in Ether you can come back to it by clicking the clock in the middle of the top bar, or by choosing **☰ → Return to Mixer** when another screen is showing.

**Plan:** Local (every plan). Streaming through Ether's own stream server needs Live 128, Live 320 or Network (see *The ON AIR button* below).

## The layout

From top to bottom:

1. **The top bar** — show name, search, the clock, and the main controls (AUTO, ON AIR, ☰).
2. **The middle** — the play queue (**Up Next**) on one side and **the board** (the faders, then **Master Out**) on the other. Drag the divider between them to change their widths. Drag a panel by its edge to swap the two sides. The small arrow at the queue's inner edge collapses the queue to a thin strip that reads **QUEUE ·** *(count)*; click the strip to open it again. When the queue is empty it says **Queue empty** and *"Drag carts here, or generate the hour in Schedule"*.
3. **The dock** — when you open a bottom-bar tab such as CATEGORIES or LIBRARY, it opens under the board. Drag the bar above it up or down to share the space.
4. **The bottom bar** — the station button, health lights, the view tabs, DELAY and DUMP.

## The top bar

**Left side**

- **Sync off** — appears only when sync is switched off on this computer. Hover for the reason; Settings turns it back on.
- **Show name** — the show on air now, in its colour. It appears only while a show is on air. Click it to return to the main screen.
- **Search** — type to search your library. On a narrow screen it becomes a magnifying-glass button that opens the Library.

**Middle**

- **The clock.** Click it to return to the main screen.

**Right side, left to right**

- **VIDEO LIVE** — appears while the Show+ video engine is live. Click it to go to Show+.
- **Upgrade** (purple, with a star) — appears when your plan does not include the cloud features. It opens your plan and subscription page.
- **The person button** — your profile. Click it to change profile (you go back to **Select your profile**; your account stays signed in).
- **AUTO / MANUAL** — the automation switch. **● AUTO** (green) means Ether is running the station. **MANUAL** means you are. **AUTO ?** with a dashed border means Ether is still waiting for the audio engine to report. See *Running the show*.
- **ON AIR / OFF AIR** — the stream button. See below.
- **☰** — the menu. See below.

### The ON AIR button

This button shows the real state of this station's stream, and starts or stops it.

- **OFF AIR** — not streaming. Click to start streaming.
- **GOING LIVE…** (amber) — connecting.
- **● ON AIR** (red) — streaming. Click to stop broadcasting.

It always shows the station you are working on. If another of your stations is streaming, this button still says OFF AIR for the station on screen.

It streams to the stream server set for the station (its Icecast settings: server, mount, password, bitrate). See *Streaming*.

**Plan:** your own Icecast server: Local (every plan). Ether's hosted stream: Live 128, Live 320 or Network.

# 3. Decks and the board

## What it's for

The board is Ether's mixing desk. It works like a broadcast console: every channel has a fader for its level and an **ON** button that opens or cuts it. Decks A, B and C play your music. Source channels carry everything else: a microphone, sweepers, carts, the jukebox, or a feed from another Ether computer. On the right, **Master Out** sets what goes to air and what you hear in the room.

## Where to find it

- **The board:** the main screen, beside the play queue.
- **The board in its own window:** **☰ → Decks**, or **Tools → Monitors → Decks**.
- **Choose deck types:** the **DECKS** tab in the bottom bar, or **View → Configure Decks...**.
- **Add a source channel:** the **+** after the last fader.
- **Master Out:** the panel at the right of the board. **Tools → Monitors → Master Output** opens it in its own window.

**Plan:** Local (every plan). Two sources use the cloud: the **Jukebox** source needs Live 128, Live 320 or Network; a **Link** paired through Ether needs Live 128, Live 320 or Network (a Link set up by typed address is Local).

## The music decks: A, B and C

Decks A, B and C are the music decks. Automation plays from them in turn, and the Library's **A**, **B** and **C** buttons load onto them.

- Each deck has its own colour: A blue, B green, C purple. The same colours mark the deck rows in Up Next and the A/B/C buttons in the Library.
- The bar at the top of a deck strip shows its role. The deck on air fills from left to right as the song plays. The deck that plays next pulses.
- Under the fader: **ON** and **PFL** (below). A channel's EQ is set in the EQ window, **☰ → Processor**: pick the channel in **Channel** at the top (see *Streaming*).

### One strip or three

The narrow **A·B·C** tab before Deck A collapses the three decks into one strip. That strip shows the deck that started most recently, and the next deck rotates in when it starts. Press **EXPAND** on the strip to show all three again. Collapsing only changes the view: all three decks keep playing, metering and responding to their faders.

## Faders

Every fader works the same way.

1. **Drag** the fader to set the level. Hold **Shift** while dragging for fine moves.
2. **Mouse wheel** or the **arrow keys** step it up or down.
3. **Double-click** sets it to 0 dB (unity).
4. Press **Enter** on a fader to type a level.

Your fader is your level. Loading a track does not move it, turning the channel OFF and ON does not move it, and Ether puts every fader back where it was after a restart. Level changes glide instead of jumping, so nothing clicks.

## ON and PFL

**ON** is the channel's on/off switch.

- On a **music deck**, **ON** starts the deck. It is the only control that starts a deck. See *Running the show* for exactly how it behaves.
- On a **source channel**, **ON** opens the channel (lit blue) or cuts it. Cutting it never moves the fader. A new source channel comes up ON.

**PFL** lets you hear a channel before it goes to air: before its fader and ON, after its EQ, in this station's local output. The programme there dips while PFL is on. PFL is never on air. Press **PFL** again to turn it off. **Settings → Audio → PFL (pre-fader listen)** sets how far the programme dips.

## Source channels

A source channel is a fader whose input you choose, like the source selector on a console. The channel is generic: what feeds it is your choice.

### Add a source channel

1. Press **+** after the last fader.
2. A new strip appears, labelled **SOURCE** with its letter, and comes up **ON**.
3. When every source channel is in use, **+** is greyed out ("Every source channel is already on the board").

### Choose what feeds it

Use the dropdown at the top of the strip:

| Choice | What it does |
|---|---|
| **— no source —** | Nothing is patched. |
| **Jukebox** | The public request jukebox plays on this channel. This channel starts **OFF**, because it faces the public. See *Jukebox*. |
| **Sweeper** | Sweepers air on this channel — the log's sweepers between songs and the ones you fire by hand. See *Imaging: sweepers, jingles and carts*. |
| **Cart / SFX rack** | Your cart wall plays on this channel. With no channel set to this, carts play on Ether's built-in cart channel. |
| **Link (network feed)** | A feed from another Ether computer comes into this fader. See *Remote Link*. |
| **Mic — pick an input…** and **INPUT DEVICES (this computer)** | A microphone on this computer. See *The mic* below. |

### DUCK

**DUCK** is the third button under the fader: **ON | PFL | DUCK**. Lit amber, it makes the music dip while this channel has audio, and rise again when it goes quiet. Not lit, the channel mixes over the music at full level. How far and how fast the music dips is set in **Settings → Audio → Ducker**.

DUCK is on every source channel, whatever feeds it. Decks A, B and C have no DUCK; the music is what gets ducked.

### Remove a source channel

Press the small **−** at the top-right of the strip. The channel leaves the board, its mic or Link is unpatched, and anything playing on it stops. If Ether cannot remove it, the strip shows the reason in red.

## The mic

A microphone is a source channel patched to a microphone. It goes on air through the same engine as the music, with its own meter, EQ, fader and ON.

1. Find a source channel, or add one with **+**.
2. In its dropdown, under **INPUT DEVICES (this computer)**, choose your microphone or audio interface. (**Mic — pick an input…** sets the channel to Mic without choosing a device yet.)
3. The line under the dropdown shows the mic's state, for example **● IN 1 · +0 dB · live**. If it shows **no input — Settings → Audio**, the mic has no input yet.
4. Bring the fader up and press **ON**.
5. To have the music dip while you talk, press **DUCK** (lit amber).

Click the state line to open **Settings → Audio**. Its **Mic Inputs (on air)** section is where you set the input number and gain. **☰ → Patch Bay** chooses the input too; it is the same setting. If a device is unplugged, the dropdown shows it as *(not connected)* and the meter reads **NOT FED** instead of a flat zero.

## Configure Decks

Open it with the **DECKS** tab or **View → Configure Decks...**.

1. Each slot is listed with its letter. Click a type to give the slot that type and turn it on:
   - **Music** — a music deck strip.
   - **Source** — a source channel with the dropdown described above.
   - **Cart** — a cart wall in that slot.
   - **Desk** — the Producer Desk in that slot.
   - **Video Studio** — Show+ in that slot.
   - **Jukebox** — offered on source slots only.
2. The switch at the right of each slot turns it on or off. Up to 6 slots can be on at once; the counter shows how many (for example **4/6**).
3. Press **Apply Layout** to save. **Cancel** closes without saving.

**Reset Default** puts the board back to the starting layout straight away: decks A, B and C on, the other slots off. Source channels you added are removed from the board.

Only decks A, B and C are played by automation and loaded by the Library's A/B/C buttons. For a mic, sweepers, carts or the jukebox, use a source channel (**+**).

## Show presets (the SHOW bar)

The bar across the top of the board saves and recalls the whole board as a named show.

1. **Arm a show…** — pick a saved show. Nothing changes yet. **▾** lists exactly what will change.
2. **TAKE ▸** *(name)* — channels that are OFF change now. Channels that are ON keep what they have, and their ON button flashes amber and says **PENDING** until you switch them OFF. **TAKE NOW** on a channel applies it immediately (its level glides, no click).
3. **DISARM** cancels an armed show.
4. **SAVE** overwrites the current show with the board as it is. **SAVE AS…** saves it under a new name. The built-in **Flat** show cannot be overwritten.

A Take never switches a channel ON. **· modified** next to the show name means the board has changed since the show was taken.

## Master Out

The panel at the right of the board.

- **Bus meters** — the programme, local, stream and monitor levels, measured after the faders.
- **Master** — the broadcast level: what listeners hear. Pull it down and the audience hears it quieter.
- **Monitor** — the level in your room. Turn it down and the room goes quiet while the station keeps broadcasting at full level.
- **Limiter** — how hard the limiter is working.
- **Processor → OPEN** — opens the EQ window (the Processor) in its own window. See *Streaming*.
- **Station** — On Air (LIVE or STBY), Uptime and Next Break. Click the heading to show or hide it.
- **Send feed** — the Remote Link sender. See *Remote Link*.
- **Station monitor strips** — a speaker level and mute for each station on this computer. These change only what you hear in the room, never the broadcast.
- The arrow at the top-left collapses Master Out to a thin meter strip; the arrow at the top opens it again. The small square-and-arrow button opens Master Out in its own window.

**MASTER is heard by everyone. MONITOR is heard by you.**

## Good to know

- **Monitor starts silent on a new computer.** Until you set it once, the Monitor fader is at off and the room is quiet. After that, Ether remembers it.
- **Master and Monitor are remembered** and put back if the audio engine restarts.
- **The board in its own window is the same board.** A fader moved in one window moves in the other.
- **A fader with no strip cannot be on air.** If the audio engine is carrying a channel the board does not list, Ether adds a strip for it so you can see and control it.
- **No key starts a deck.** Only ON does.

# 4. Running the show

## What it's for

This chapter covers the moment-to-moment work of being on air: letting Ether run the station (AUTO) or running it yourself (MANUAL), reading what is coming up, putting songs on the decks, starting a deck, and using the broadcast delay to keep something off the air.

## Where to find it

- **AUTO / MANUAL:** the button in the top bar, left of ON AIR. Keyboard: **A**.
- **Up Next (the play queue):** beside the board on the main screen. **Tools → Monitors → Queue / Up Next** opens it in its own window.
- **Loading a deck:** the **A**, **B** and **C** buttons in the Library.
- **Starting a deck:** the deck's **ON** button on the board.
- **CLEAR ▾:** the bottom bar, when the queue has items.
- **DELAY** and **DUMP:** the right side of the bottom bar.

**Plan:** Local (every plan).

## AUTO and MANUAL

**AUTO** means Ether runs the station: it follows the Program Log, cues the next songs on the decks, plays them in turn, and fires spots and sweepers. **MANUAL** means you run it.

The button always shows the current state:

- **● AUTO** (green) — automation is on.
- **MANUAL** — you are in control.
- **AUTO ?** (dashed border) — Ether is waiting for the audio engine to report. Click it to turn AUTO on.

### Turn AUTO on

1. Click **MANUAL** (or press **A**).
2. The button turns green and reads **● AUTO**. Ether loads the queue from the Program Log, starting from the song scheduled for now, and starts playing.

**If nothing is scheduled for the next two hours,** Ether is meant to ask: "Nothing is scheduled to play right now. Auto-populate the queue with rotation-eligible songs?" **OK** fills the queue with songs from your rotation and starts. **Cancel** — or no question at all — leaves the button on **MANUAL** and opens the Program Log under the board, so you can build the day with Fill Day (see *The schedule and the Program Log*).

### Turn AUTO off

1. Click **● AUTO** (or press **A**).
2. The button reads **MANUAL**. The song on air finishes. After it, nothing advances on its own: no next song, no spots, no sweepers.

In MANUAL, nothing is cued for you. You load and start every deck yourself.

## Up Next

Up Next is what plays after the song on air, in the Program Log's order.

**The deck rows (top).** Three rows, one per music deck, in the deck colours (A blue, B green, C purple):

- The deck **on air** fills from left to right as the song plays, and shows the time played and the song's length.
- The deck **playing next** pulses.
- In the **last 10 seconds** of the song on air, its row flashes and the time turns amber, so you know when to talk.
- A deck holding a **spot** has a pulsing amber frame until the spot ends.
- A sweeper due on a seam shows as its own row (**SWP**) above the song it introduces. If its audio is not on this computer, it says **FILE MISSING — WILL NOT PLAY**.

**The list (below).** The songs still to come, each with its title, artist and length. Spots carry a **SPOT** badge. Scheduled announcements appear at the time they will fire. A song placed earlier than your separation rules allow shows **⚠ separation ·** *(minutes)* **early**.

When the queue is empty, it says **Queue empty**.

### Take a song out

1. Find the song in Up Next.
2. Click its **✕** (or right-click it and choose **Remove**).

The song comes out of the Program Log too, not just off the list, so it does not come back and does not air. If it was already cued on a deck, the deck is emptied and cued with the next song. The song on air cannot be removed here; use its deck's **ON** button. A row that already aired is the record of what happened and is never touched.

Right-clicking a song also offers the same song actions you get on a deck or in the Library (such as edit and show in library).

To change the running order of later songs, edit the Program Log. Up Next is re-read from the log every few seconds.

### CLEAR ▾

When the queue has items, **CLEAR ▾** appears in the bottom bar. It offers:

- **Reset to schedule** — re-syncs playout and re-cues the idle decks from the log.
- **Clear & regenerate** — rewrites the log from now on. The hour in progress is spared.

## Loading a deck

1. Open the Library (the **LIBRARY** tab, **☰ → Library**, or press **L**).
2. Find the song.
3. Press **A**, **B** or **C** on its row (or right-click it and choose **Load to Deck A**, **B** or **C**).
4. While it loads, a note says **Loading "**…**" onto** *(deck)***…**.

**Q** on the row (or **Add to Queue**) adds the song to the bottom of Up Next instead. It becomes a row of the Program Log added by hand, and nothing automatic removes it.

**Ether never loads over a deck that is on air.** If the deck is playing or paused, nothing is loaded and a note says: **Deck** *(X)* **is on air — "**…**" was not loaded. Stop deck** *(X)* **first.** If the song's file cannot be found, the note says so (for example **unavailable, needs re-import**).

## Starting a deck: ON

**ON is how you put a deck on air, and it is the only button that does it.**

### Start a cued deck

1. Make sure the deck has a song on it (the deck shows a title).
2. Press that deck's **ON** on the board.
3. The deck goes on air. If another deck was playing, it comes off almost at once.

You do not need to stop the other deck first, and you do not need to leave AUTO. In AUTO, the next songs re-cue behind you and the log carries on from the song you started.

### Turn a deck off

Press **ON** on the deck that is playing. The deck goes silent at once. This is a stop, not a pause: the deck goes back to idle. To hear that song again, load it again.

### Start from silence

If nothing is playing, press **ON** on any cued deck. It starts.

### How ON behaves

- **The takeover is a quick cut, not a fade.** When you press ON to take over, the outgoing song is cut after about a third of a second. You reach for ON in a hurry for a wrong song, a bad file or bad language, and that audio needs to be gone. Automatic song-to-song segues in AUTO are not affected; they keep their normal overlap.
- **ON does not touch your faders.**
- **Pressing twice does no harm.** A second press on a deck that is already starting is ignored. You cannot put two decks on air by pressing quickly.
- **An empty deck does not start.** Load a song first.

## Esc

**Esc closes things. It never stops audio.** It closes the ☰ menu, the shortcut list, Configure Decks, dialogs and open lists. Nothing on air changes when you press it.

## The broadcast delay: DELAY and DUMP

The broadcast delay holds your stream a few seconds behind what you hear in the studio. If something goes wrong — bad language from a caller, say — you can press **DUMP** to throw that audio away before listeners hear it.

The delay is on the **stream** only. Your studio speakers stay live, so you hear everything first.

### Arm the delay

1. Press **DELAY** in the bottom bar.
2. It turns amber and reads **DELAY 8s**. A small bar beside it fills as the delay builds up.
3. When the bar is full (it turns green), the stream is 8 seconds behind the studio and **DUMP** is ready.

### Dump

1. Press **DUMP** (red when ready).
2. The audio held in the delay is dropped and the stream jumps to live.
3. The delay stays armed and builds back up by itself. **DUMP** is ready again when the bar is full.

**DUMP** is greyed out until the delay is armed and full. Hover for the reason: **Arm the delay first** or **Delay still building…**.

### Disarm the delay

Press **DELAY** again. The stream goes back to no delay.

## Good to know

- **No key starts, pauses or resumes a deck.** Only the board's **ON** does. **A** turns AUTO on or off; it does not play anything.
- **The delay is 8 seconds.** It is not adjustable in this version.
- **Every start is recorded.** The Health Monitor's live activity log shows each start, segue and stop with the song name.

# 5. Library

## What it's for

The Library is every piece of audio your station owns: songs, spots, sweepers and announcements. You bring
audio in here, give each song a category so it can be scheduled, fix its title and artist, set its cue points,
and load it to a deck or the queue. Every file you import is copied into Ether's own audio folder, so the
Library never depends on where the file happened to sit on your computer.

## Where to find it

- **Library → Library** opens the Library as a full screen in the main window.
- **LIBRARY** in the bottom bar opens it docked under the decks, so the live screen stays in view. Drag the
  divider above it to make it taller or shorter.
- **☰ → Library** opens it in its own window. (The cue editor items are not in this window. Use the main
  window's Library to edit cue points.)
- **File → Import Music…** and **Library → Import from Folder…** both open the Library screen. Then press
  **+ Import Music** (see below).

**Plan:** Local (every plan). The cloud music library at the end of this chapter needs **Live 128, Live 320 or
Network**.

## Import music

1. Open the Library and press **+ Import Music** (top right).
2. Under **Assign to Category**, pick the category the new songs belong to, or leave **No Category**.
   To make a new category here, type a **Code (e.g. XMAS)** and a **Name (e.g. Christmas)** and press **+ New**.
3. Press **Choose Folder & Import** to bring in a whole folder (sub-folders included), or **Choose File(s) &
   Import** to pick single files. Ether accepts MP3, FLAC, OGG, WAV, M4A, AAC and AIFF.
4. Ether first checks for songs you already have. A song counts as "already in your library" when it has the
   **same title and the same length** as one you own.
   - **Skip all … — import only the … new** leaves your songs as they are.
   - **Replace all …** keeps your songs (their category and play history) and swaps in the new files.
   - Or choose **Skip** or **Replace** song by song, then press **Continue**.
   - **Cancel import** goes back without importing anything.
5. When it finishes, **Import Complete** shows how many were imported, skipped and replaced. If any file
   could not be copied into the audio folder, it is listed under **NOT added**, and no entry was made for it.
6. Press **Done**.

Ether measures the loudness of each new song in the background after import.

## Find a song

- **Search title or artist…** also matches cart numbers.
- The type chips (**Songs**, **Spots**, **Sweepers**, **Announcements**) narrow the list to one kind of audio.
  With none picked, you see everything.
- **UNMEASURED** (only shown when there are some) lists songs that have no loudness measurement yet. They play
  at the file's own level.
- **All Categories** shows one category at a time.
- Click a column heading to sort by it. Click again to reverse.
- **⚙** (at the right of the column headings) chooses which columns you see.

## Give songs a category

A song with no category can never be scheduled. Its category box is outlined in amber and says **WON'T AIR**.

1. In the song's **Category** column, pick a category from the drop-down. It saves at once.
2. To make a new category from the Library, press **+ Category**, type a **Code** (up to 6 letters) and a
   **Name**, pick a colour and press **Create**.

## Edit a song's details

- **Double-click** a cell in the **Artist**, **Album**, **Year**, **Genre** or **BPM** column, type, and press
  **Enter** (Esc cancels). If the save is refused, the cell stays open and says why.
- For the title and everything at once, **right-click** the song and choose **Edit Metadata**. Change
  **Title**, **Artist**, **Album**, **Year**, **Genre** or **BPM** and press **Save**.
- **🔍 Lookup on Discogs** (in Edit Metadata) searches Discogs and fills artist, album, year and genre from the
  result you click. It needs your Discogs key, entered in **Settings → Integrations → Discogs Metadata**.
- **Right-click → Enter Cart #** gives the item a short number (for example 1001, J14, TALK7). Ether warns you
  if another item already has that number.

## Play, queue and other actions

Each row has quick buttons:

- **A**, **B**, **C** load the song to that deck.
- **Q** adds it to the bottom of the queue.
- The **cue** button opens a small menu: **Open in Cue Editor** or **Quick Cue here** (a compact cue editor that
  slides up over the Library).
- **✕** deletes it (Ether asks first).

**Queue All** adds every song in the current view to the queue.

Tick the boxes at the left of rows to select several. With two or more selected, **Bulk assign metadata**
appears. **Delete N** deletes the selected items (Ether asks first and reports how many went).

**Right-click** a row for more:

- **Mark as Sweeper** / **Unmark Sweeper (→ Music)** — moves it out of (or back into) music rotation as imaging.
- **Mark as Spot (SPOT)** — turns the track into a spot. A small window asks for a spot **Category** (pick one or
  type a new name) and a **Type** (Commercial, Promo, PSA, Sponsorship). The track leaves music rotation and
  appears in Spots & Promos (Chapter 8). **Unmark Spot (→ Music)** puts it back.
- **Load to Deck A / B / C**, **Add to Queue**.
- **Edit Cue Points** — opens the Cue Editor.
- **Measure loudness** / **Re-measure loudness** — measures the file and stores the result. The audio file
  itself is never changed.
- **Send to Studio** — opens the song in the Show+ DAW window.
- **Open File Location** — shows the file on disk.
- **Delete**.

## Normalize

**Normalize** (in the row of buttons at the top of the Library) works out, for each song, how much it would need
to be turned up or down to reach −14 LUFS, and stores that figure with the song. The audio file is not changed.

1. Press **Normalize**.
2. Ether takes up to 50 songs that don't have a figure yet and works through them. The status reads
   **Analyzing... N/50** as it goes.
3. When it finishes it says **Done! Analyzed N songs.** If every song already has a figure, it says **All songs
   already analyzed**.
4. If a song can't be analyzed (for example the file is missing or can't be read), Ether says so and leaves that
   song exactly as it was. No made-up figure is stored. The message reads **Analyzed X of N. K could not be
   analyzed and were left unchanged:** followed by the first few song titles. It stays on screen longer so you can
   read it.
5. Press **Normalize** again for the next 50.

The level a song plays at on air comes from its loudness measurement (see **UNMEASURED** and **Measure
loudness** above), not from the Normalize figure.

## Fix missing song lengths (Scan Durations)

A song with no length, or a length under one second, throws off clock timing. **⏱ Scan Durations** reads each
such file's real length.

1. Open the **CATEGORIES** editor (Chapter 6).
2. Press **⏱ Scan Durations** (next to **+ New Category**). It reads **Scanning...** while it works.
3. The status beside it counts up: **Scanning... N/M (X fixed, Y failed)**.
4. When it finishes: **✓ Done — X updated, Y failed**. A song counts as failed when its file can't be read; its
   length is left as it was. If every song already has a length, it says **✓ All songs have duration data**.

## Delete

Deleting takes the item out of the library and out of every future log. What already aired stays in your
records. Ether always asks first; press **Delete** (or Enter) to confirm, **Cancel** (or Esc) to keep it.

**Delete All** removes every song in this station's library. Ether asks first. Use it with care.

## Cue Editor

The Cue Editor sets where a song starts and ends on air, and where its intro and outro are.

**Where to find it:** right-click a song → **Edit Cue Points**, or the row's cue button → **Open in Cue
Editor** / **Quick Cue here**. **Library → Cue Editor** and **Tools → Cue Editor** open it too: if no song is
loaded in it, you see **Import Tracks**, where **Browse Files** or **Scan Folder** adds audio and **Edit cues →**
opens a song.

**Plan:** Local (every plan).

1. Press play (or **Space**) to listen. The preview plays on the output chosen in the box at the top
   (**Default Output** unless you pick another). It does not go to air.
2. Click anywhere on the waveform to move the playhead there.
3. Drag the markers on the waveform to set:
   - **CUE IN** — where playback starts.
   - **INTRO END** — where the vocals come in (for talking over the intro).
   - **OUTRO START** — where the next item may start.
   - **CUE OUT** — where playback ends.
4. Or press **INTRO** or **OUTRO**, then drag across the waveform to paint that region.
5. Click one of the four boxes above the waveform to jump to that point. **▶ FROM IN** plays from Cue In.
6. Zoom with the zoom buttons or Ctrl+scroll. Scroll to move along the song. **FIT** shows the whole track.
7. Press **Save Cue Points**. The button shows **✓ Saved**.

Also in the top bar:

- **E** / **🔞 EXPLICIT** marks the song explicit or clean (saved with **Save Cue Points**).
- **MB Lookup** looks the song up on MusicBrainz and marks it explicit or clean from what it finds (it saves
  straight away).
- **Reset** puts the four points back to the start and end of the file. Nothing is saved until you press
  **Save Cue Points**.

## Clip Editor (video clips)

**Where to find it:** **Tools → Clip Editor**.

**Plan:** Local (every plan).

The Clip Editor records short video clips from your camera, sized for broadcast or social media.

1. Ether opens your camera. If it can't, it says **Camera unavailable**; press **Retry**.
2. Under **FORMAT** pick **16:9 Landscape**, **9:16 Vertical** or **1:1 Square**, or pick a phone or tablet
   under **DEVICE PRESETS**.
3. Press **⏺ Record**. Press **Stop** to finish.
4. Watch it back. **📤 Save to Disk** saves it as a WebM video. **↩ Back to Camera** returns to the camera.
   **✕ Discard** throws the clip away.
5. **📂 Open Video…** plays an existing video file in the same viewer.

## Import Library (from another automation system)

**Where to find it:** **Tools → Import Library…**

**Plan:** Local (every plan).

This brings in a **song list** from WideOrbit, GSelector or Zetta (XML or CSV export).

1. Optionally pick the source system. Ether also detects it from the file.
2. Drop the XML or CSV file on the box, or click it to browse.
3. Check the field mapping table (**Source field**, **Sample value**, **Maps to**, **Status**).
4. Press **Start Import — N records**. Songs already in your library (same title and artist) are skipped. Categories named
   in the file are created if you don't have them.
5. **Import complete** shows what was imported and skipped. Press **Done**.

This imports the song **records only**: title, artist, length, category, rotation details. It does not bring in
any audio. A song with no audio file cannot air.

## Cloud music library

**Where to find it:** **Settings → Backup & Restore → Keep my stuff synced**, then open **Advanced**.

**Plan:** Live 128, Live 320 or Network.

Your audio files can be kept in the cloud so every computer signed into your account has them.

- **Coming down is automatic.** While **Keep my stuff synced** is on, Ether checks for new audio on a timer
  and fetches only the files this computer doesn't have yet.
- **Going up is a button.** Audio you add on this computer goes up when you press **Send just the audio**.
  Nothing uploads on a timer. **Stop** cancels a send in progress. Tick **Re-send every file, even ones already
  uploaded** to send everything again.
- **Your audio files** says how many of your files are in the cloud and how many are still to send.
- **WHERE YOUR AUDIO LIVES** shows the folder Ether keeps your audio in. **Change folder** picks another.
- **Having trouble? Check connection** tests the link to the cloud.

## Good to know

- Every import copies the file into Ether's audio folder and stores that copy. If a file can't be copied, Ether
  tells you which one and why, and makes no entry for it.
- A song is only scheduled if it has a category, has an audio file, and is marked as music (not a sweeper or
  spot).
- If the library is borrowed from another account, it is read-only: **+ Import Music** shows **🔒 Borrowed
  (read-only)** and title/artist/album/year/genre/BPM can't be changed. You can still give songs categories and
  program them.
- Deleting a song never rewrites what already aired.

# 6. Categories, clocks and shows

## What it's for

These three things decide what your station plays and when.

- A **category** is a group of songs that rotate together (for example A = power currents, D = gold).
- A **clock** is the recipe for one hour: which category plays in each position, where the spots go, where the
  talk breaks are.
- A **show** is a block of time on certain days (a daypart) with a clock assigned to it.

When you fill the log (Chapter 7), Ether walks through your shows hour by hour, follows each show's clock, and
picks a song from each category position using your rotation rules.

## Where to find it

- **Bottom bar:** **SHOWS**, **CLOCKS** and **CATEGORIES** each open that editor docked under the decks.
  Press the same button again to close it.
- **Schedule → Clocks**, **Schedule → Shows & Dayparts**, **Schedule → Categories** open the **Schedule**
  screen in the main window on that tab. It has all three tabs and a **+ Create Show** button.
- **☰ → Shows**, **☰ → Categories**, and **☰ → Schedule** (clocks) open each editor in its own window.
- **Schedule → Schedule Manager** shows all three side by side (Chapter 7).
- **Rotation rules:** **Settings → Programming → Music Scheduling Rules**.

**Plan:** Local (every plan).

## Categories

### Make a category

1. Open **CATEGORIES** and press **+ New Category**.
2. Type a **Code** (for example A) and a **Name**.
3. Optionally enter **Spins/hr** (your target, see below) and pick a colour.
4. Press **Save**. It shows **✓ Saved**.

### Change a category

Press **Edit** on its row, change it, and press **Save**. **Cancel** closes without saving.

The list shows each category's colour, code, name, how many **Songs** are in it, and its **Spins/hr** target.

### What Spins/hr does

**Spins/hr** is a target you declare: how many times an hour you want this category heard. It does not change
which songs get picked. Rotation Analytics compares what actually aired against it, and the Health Monitor uses
it to warn when a category has too few songs. Leaving it empty means "no target".

### Put songs into a category

Songs get their category in the Library (Chapter 5): the category drop-down on each row, or **Assign to
Category** while importing.

## Clocks

### Make a clock

1. Open **CLOCKS**.
2. Type a name in **New clock name...** and press **Create**. The new clock is selected.

**SAVED CLOCKS** on the left lists your clocks. Click one to open it. To delete one, press **✕** next to it,
then **Yes** (or **No** to keep it). Deleting a clock also removes it from any show that used it.

### Build the hour

The clock is a list of rows from the top of the hour down. Each row shows its **POSITION** (minutes and seconds
past the hour), **TYPE**, **CATEGORY**, a sample **TITLE** and **ARTIST** from that category, and its **DURATION**.
The bottom line says how full the hour is: **N min remaining**, **✓ Hour complete**, or **⚠ … over — remove
segments**.

Add rows with **QUICK ADD** at the top:

- Click a **category code** to add a song row from that category. Its length is set to the average length of the
  songs in that category.
- **Spots** → pick a spot category (or **Any spot**) to add a spot row. One spot from that category airs there.
- **TALK** → pick a length (**:15**, **:30**, **1:00**, **1:30**, **2:00**, **3:00**) or type a **CUSTOM** length
  and press **Add**.

**+ More Options** opens the same choices step by step (**Song**, **Spots**, **Talk break**).

### Change a row

- **Double-click** the TYPE or CATEGORY cell to change it. On a Spots row, pick which spot category airs there.
- **Click** a row to select it, then type a new length in its DURATION box and press **Enter**.
- **Drag** a row up or down to move it.
- **📌** pins the row to one exact item by its cart number: type the **Cart #** and press OK. That item then
  plays in that position every time. Leave the box blank to clear the pin.
- **⎘** duplicates the row. **✕** removes it.
- With a row selected, **Ctrl+C** copies it and **Ctrl+V** adds a copy at the end of the clock.

Changes save as you make them. They reach the air the next time the log is filled (**Fill Day** in the Program
Log, Chapter 7).

### Timed spot breaks

Below the clock is **Timed Spot Breaks**: spots at set minutes past the hour, with music filling around them.

1. Press **+ Add break**.
2. In **Min past hr**, type the minute (0–59). **:00** lands exactly at the top of the hour. Any other minute
   lands at the nearest song boundary; a song is never cut.
3. Pick the **Spot category** (the number in brackets is how many spots can air from it today) or **Any spot**.
4. In **Spots**, type how many spots play back to back (1–10).
5. **✕** removes a break. Changes save automatically (**✓ Saved** flashes).

If a break has nothing it can air, Ether says so under it: **⚠ 0 eligible spots — this break airs nothing**, with
the reason.

**Important:** when a clock has any timed breaks, Ether uses only that clock's **song rows** plus the timed
breaks. The clock's Spots rows and talk breaks are not placed, and 📌 pins are not used. If you want those,
use a clock without timed breaks.

### Spot categories (on the clock screen)

In the full Schedule screen and the CLOCKS editor, **Spot Categories** lists your spot categories. **Edit** renames
or recolours one (**Save**). To add one, type in **New category…**, pick a colour and press **Add**. The same list
is in Spots & Promos (Chapter 8).

## Shows and dayparts

### Make a show

1. Open **SHOWS** and press **+ New Show**.
2. Type the **Show name**.
3. Set when it **starts** and **ends**. Any minute works (a show ending at 1:30 automates until 1:30). An end of
   00:00 means midnight.
4. Pick a colour and, if you like, a **Description**.
5. Click the **Days** it runs (blue = on).
6. Leave **Active** ticked.
7. Press **Save Show**.

### Give a show its clock

In the show list, pick a clock in the show's **Clock** drop-down. It saves at once. **— none —** removes it.

The **24-Hour Timeline** at the top draws every show in its colour, so gaps and overlaps are easy to see.

Press **Edit** on a show's row to change it, then **Save Show**.

### Create Show (step by step)

On the full Schedule screen, **+ Create Show** walks you through it:

1. **Name & Daypart** — name, start and end time, active days, colour.
2. **Format Clock** — pick an existing clock, or create a new one by name.
3. **Music Category** — you can create a new category here (code, name, colour).
4. **Review** — check it and press **✓ Create Show**.

## Rotation rules

**Where:** **Settings → Programming → Music Scheduling Rules**.

These rules decide how soon things can repeat, and how many songs in a row can come from one category. Ether
checks every song it picks against everything around it in the log, including what already aired.

- **Same song plays again after** — minutes (or hours) before the exact same recording can play again.
- **Same artist plays again after** — minutes before the same artist can play again. An artist credit such as
  "A feat. B" or "A & B" counts for both artists.
- **Same title (different artist) after** — minutes before the same title can play again. Two songs only count
  as the same title when the title **and** the length (to the second) match.
- **Max songs in a row from same category** — how many songs from one category can follow each other.

For each rule:

1. Type the number.
2. For the three time rules, pick **min** or **hrs**. (The max-in-a-row rule counts **songs**.)
3. Press the **STRICT** / **SOFT** button to switch between the two (see below).
4. Use the switch at the right to turn the rule on or off (a rule that is off is dimmed).

Each change saves at once.

### STRICT and SOFT

When Ether fills a music position, it looks for a song in that category that meets every rule that is on (then in
the station's fallback category, if you named one). If none does:

1. **SOFT rules are relaxed first.** Ether picks from the songs that still meet every **STRICT** rule, and records
   with that log row which SOFT rule was bent.
2. **A STRICT rule is broken only as the last resort**, when no song can meet it. The position then gets the song in
   that category that has waited longest since it last played. This is recorded with the log row and listed in the
   Health Monitor.

A position is only **UNSCHEDULED** when its category has no playable songs at all.

While the log is playing, Ether also refuses a song that would break a **STRICT** rule against what has really aired.
SOFT rules are only used when the log is filled.

### Max songs in a row

When a position would make one song too many from the same category:

- **STRICT**: that position takes its song from the next different category in the clock instead. If there is no
  other category in the clock, or it has no song, the rule is broken as the last resort, recorded with the log row
  and in the Health Monitor.
- **SOFT**: the position keeps its category, and the log row records that the rule was broken.

Only songs count. Spots, talk breaks and other items between songs don't end a run. The count starts again each
time the log is filled.

The box under the rules on the Settings page sums this up.

## Good to know

- A song is only picked if it has a category, has an audio file, is marked as music, and is not set inactive.
- Clock changes, show changes and rule changes affect the log the next time it is filled. Rows that already
  aired never change.
- A talk-break row holds its time in the hour. No audio is placed for it.

# 7. The schedule and the Program Log

## What it's for

The **Program Log** is the day's log your station actually airs from: every song, spot and break, hour by
hour, with what already played, what is on air and what is coming. You build it with **Fill Day** or **Fill
Week**, which follow your shows, clocks, categories and rotation rules (Chapter 6). You can then change any hour
by hand.

The **Schedule Manager** puts shows, clocks, categories, spots, sweepers, the Program Log and the analytics on
one screen. **Rotation Analytics** shows what actually aired against your targets.

## Where to find it

- **Program Log**
  - **PROGRAM LOG** in the bottom bar docks it under the decks (the live screen stays in view).
  - **Schedule → Program Log** or **☰ → Program Log** opens it in its own window.
  - You can have both open. They show the same rows and update each other within about a second.
- **Schedule Manager:** **Schedule → Schedule Manager** or **☰ → Schedule Manager**.
- **Rotation Analytics:** **Schedule → Rotation Analytics** or **☰ → Rotation Analytics**.

**Plan:** Local (every plan), except the **PDF Report** export (see "Export the log").

## Read the day

1. Pick a day on the small calendar at the left (**‹** and **›** change the month). A dot under a day means it
   already has a log.
2. **TODAY'S SHOWS** lists the day's shows. Click one to show only its hours; click it again to show all.
3. The day header reads **N of M hours scheduled · total programming**.
4. Each hour is a row: the show, the clock, how many items and how long they run. A green dot means the hour is
   filled; red means it has an empty position. Click the row to open or close it. **Expand All** and **Collapse
   All** do every hour.
5. Inside an hour, each item shows **Time**, **Type**, **Title**, **Artist**, **Duration** and **Status**.
   - **Time** is the scheduled time. Once the item has aired, the actual air time appears under it in green.
   - **Status** is **pending** (not yet), **playing** (on air now), **played**, or **didn't play**.
6. **Click a song** to see **Why this song**: its category, how many songs were in the pool, which rules ruled
   others out, and whether a rule had to be bent. Click again to hide it.

## Fill Day and Fill Week

**⚡ Fill Day** builds the log for the selected day from your shows and clocks.

- It never changes anything that played, is playing, or was marked didn't play.
- An hour that has already started playing is never touched.
- Items you placed or changed by hand (marked **YOURS** in the hour editor) are kept.
- If the day has fully aired, it says **Nothing to fill — the day has aired**.
- When it finishes, the message says how many rows it built, how many positions got the least recently played
  song because nothing passed your rules, and how many are **UNSCHEDULED** (their category has no playable songs).

**⚡ Fill Week** does the same for the selected day **and the six days after it**, in one run. To plan further
ahead, pick a later start day and press Fill Week again. There is no limit on how far ahead.

While either one runs, a progress bar shows how far it has got. **CANCEL** stops it at the next hour boundary.
Every day that already finished is kept; you never get half a day.

Fill Day and Fill Week act on the station you are switched into.

**Each show's log is also built automatically before the show starts**, so there is always music inside a show
even if nobody pressed Fill Day.

## Clear

- **Clear Day** removes everything that hasn't aired yet, from the next hour on. Played, playing and didn't-play
  rows stay.
- The **✕** on an hour row clears that one hour. It only appears on hours that haven't started.

To rebuild part of a day, clear the hours you want rebuilt and press **Fill Day**. It fills the gaps and leaves
the rest in place.

## Edit an hour

1. Press **✎ Edit** on a filled hour. The hour editor opens.
2. **Swap a song:** click a song. A list of songs from the same category appears; type in **Search songs...** to
   narrow it, then click the song you want.
3. **Swap two times:** drag a row onto another row. The two trade places.
4. **Remove a row:** press **✕** on it. The position refills on the next Fill Day.
5. Close the editor with **✕** at the top right.

Anything you place, move or swap is marked **YOURS**. Fill Day and Fill Week will not move, replace or remove it.

If an edit bends one of your rotation rules, a short amber note under the row says which. It informs; it does not
block.

Rows that already aired can't be changed. Clicking one says **Already aired — a record, not a plan**. If Ether
refuses an edit for any other reason, the reason appears in red at the top of the editor.

## Export the log

- **⬇ CSV** saves the day as a spreadsheet file.
- **🖨 Print** opens a printable log and the print dialog.
- **📄 PDF Report** builds a traffic report (station name, day totals, every hour) and opens the print dialog,
  where you can save it as a PDF. **Plan:** (not in the price list).

## Shows & Dayparts (from the Program Log)

**⚙ Shows & Dayparts** in the day header opens a list of your shows where you can set each show's **Format
Clock**.

To delete a show here, press the **✕** at the right of its row. It is deleted straight away (Ether does not ask
first) and disappears from the list and the timeline.

For everything else about shows, use the Shows editor (Chapter 6).

## Schedule Manager

The Schedule Manager is one screen made of panes: **Shows**, **Clocks**, **Categories**, **Spots**, **Sweepers**,
**Rotation Analytics**, **Program Log** and **Play Log**. Each pane is the same editor you get elsewhere, so
everything in Chapters 6, 7 and 8 works the same here.

1. The header shows how many shows, clocks and categories this station has.
2. **Drag** a pane by its tab to move it, stack it with others, or dock it beside another. Drag the edges between
   panes to resize them.
3. Close a pane with its **✕**. **Panels** brings it back; when panes are closed it reads **Panels (N hidden)**.
4. Under **Layouts** in the Panels menu, pick a ready arrangement:
   - **Programming** — build the hour: shows, clocks, and the categories they draw from.
   - **Traffic** — spot work: spot categories and the breaks that place them.
   - **Analysis** — what actually aired, next to the targets and clocks that asked for it.
   - **Live** — today's log beside the shows that built it.
5. **Reset layout** returns to the Programming arrangement.
6. **Fixed layout** switches to a simpler three-pane screen. **Rotation Analytics →** opens Rotation Analytics.

Clicking a show selects its clock in the Clocks pane. Clicking a category outlines (in amber) every clock that
uses it.

## Rotation Analytics

Rotation Analytics reports what actually aired. It changes nothing.

1. Pick **24 Hours**, **7 Days** or **30 Days**. **Refresh** reads it again.
2. **Spins per hour — actual vs target** — each category's spins per hour against its **Spins/hr** target. A
   category with no target shows **—**.
3. **Hourly grid** — spins per category per hour.
4. **Artist burn** — the closest two airings of each artist, compared with your artist rule. **INSIDE RULE**
   marks an artist that played closer than your rule allows.
5. **Turnover** — how much of each category's eligible songs actually aired. Spins per song near 1.0 is even
   rotation.
6. **Why was this picked?** — how many aired rows carry a recorded reason. **Program Log →** opens the Program
   Log, where clicking a song shows its reason.

Each table has **Export CSV**. Column headings sort; column widths are remembered.

## Good to know

- The Program Log is the same log the station plays from. What you see is what airs.
- What aired is a record. Nothing in this chapter can change a played row.
- A reason is only recorded when a row is built. Rows built before reasons were kept say so; a reason is never
  made up afterwards.
- A timed break whose minute the clock's songs never reach is not placed. It is reported to the Health Monitor so
  you can fix the clock.

# 8. Spots and promos

## What it's for

**Spots & Promos** is where your commercials, promos and PSAs live, with their advertiser, dates, daily limit and
category. Your clocks then say where spots go in each hour, and Fill Day picks which spot airs in each break,
rotating them so the one that has waited longest goes next.

## Where to find it

- **SPOTS** in the bottom bar.
- **Library → Spots & Promos**.
- The **Spots** pane in the **Schedule Manager** (Chapter 7).

Where spots air is set on the clock: **CLOCKS** → **Timed Spot Breaks**, or a **Spots** row in the clock
(Chapter 6).

**Plan:** Local (every plan).

## Add spots

At the top of Spots & Promos:

1. **Add Files** — pick one or more audio files (MP3, FLAC, OGG, WAV, M4A, AAC).
2. **Import Folder** — pick a folder; every audio file in it is added (sub-folders are not searched).

Each file is copied into Ether's audio folder and becomes a spot named after the file, with its real length read
from the file. A file that is already a spot is skipped, and the message says how many.

You can also turn a track that is already in the Library into a spot: in the Library, **right-click → Mark as Spot
(SPOT)**, pick a category and a type, and confirm (Chapter 5).

### Import Traffic CSV

**Import Traffic CSV** reads a CSV or TSV export from a traffic system. Ether finds the columns by their headings:
title, ISCI or cart number, advertiser, agency, length, start date, end date and type. A row that matches an
existing spot (same ISCI, or same title and advertiser) updates that spot's dates and agency and switches it back
to active; other rows become new spots.

This brings in the spot **records**, not audio. A spot with no audio file is never placed in the log and is
marked **⚠ WON'T AIR**. To give it audio, open it with **Edit** and press **Attach audio…** (see "Edit a spot").

## Spot categories

A spot category groups spots so a break can pull from it (for example "Weekday Sponsors" or "PSAs").

1. Under **Spot Categories**, type a name in **New category name…**, pick a colour, and press **Add Category** (or
   Enter).
2. **Edit** renames or recolours a category; press **Save**.

The category list at the top (**Category: All / … / Uncategorized**) filters the spot list. The number after each
name is how many spots are in it.

## Edit a spot

1. Press **Edit** on the spot's row.
2. Set:
   - **Title** and type (**promo**, **psa**, **jingle**, **liner**, **sweeper**, **commercial**, **imaging**).
   - **Audio** — the name of the spot's audio file. A spot with none reads **No audio — this spot can't air until
     you attach its audio**. Press **Attach audio…** (or **Replace audio…** when it already has one) and pick an
     audio file (MP3, FLAC, OGG, WAV, M4A, AAC). Ether copies it into its audio folder, reads its real length, and
     saves it to the spot straight away; the message says **Audio attached to "…"**.
   - **Spot category** — the category a break pulls it from. **— No category (won't be pulled by a timed break)
     —** means no break will pull it, including **Any spot** breaks.
   - **Advertiser**.
   - The two date boxes beside Advertiser are the **start** and **end date** — the spot only airs between these
     dates. Leave them empty for no limit.
   - **Cart #** and **ISCI**.
   - **Max plays/day** — the most times Fill Day places it in one day.
   - **Active** — switch it off to take the spot off the air.
   - **Notes**.
   - **Artwork** — **Choose image…** picks a picture shown as this spot's artwork while it plays, instead of
     whatever artwork the file carries. **Clear**
     removes it.
3. Press **Save**. **Cancel** closes without saving.

Switching a spot to inactive takes it out of every future log. The message says how many future airings were
pulled.

## The spot list

- The type chips (**All**, **promo**, **commercial**, …) filter by type.
- The counters show **Total Spots**, **Active** and **Total Plays**.
- Each row shows **Title**, **Type**, **Category**, **Advertiser**, **Plays** and **Active**.
- **⚠ WON'T AIR** marks a spot that is inactive, has no category, or has no audio. Hover over it to see which.
  No break ever picks a spot marked WON'T AIR, including **Any spot** breaks and rows.
- **▶ Play** loads the spot on **Deck B and starts it at once**. That is on air. Use it only when you mean to play
  the spot now.
- **Q** adds the spot to the bottom of the queue.
- **Edit** opens the editor.

## Put spots in the hour

There are two ways, both on the clock (Chapter 6):

- **Timed Spot Breaks** — "at :20, play 3 spots from Weekday Sponsors". The break lands at the nearest song
  boundary to that minute (a song is never cut); **:00** lands exactly at the top of the hour.
- **A Spots row** in the clock — one spot from the chosen spot category plays in that position.

A clock that has timed breaks uses only the timed breaks for spots; its Spots rows are not placed.

**Any spot** (on a break or a row) means any active spot in any of your spot categories. A spot with no
category is not included.

### Which spot airs

Each time a break needs a spot, Fill Day picks from the spots that are:

- active,
- in that category (or in any category, for **Any spot** — a spot with no category is never picked),
- inside their start and end dates,
- holding an audio file,
- under their **Max plays/day** for that day,

and takes the one that has waited longest since it last aired.

### When a break would air nothing

If a timed break on this station's active clock has no spot it can air, Spots & Promos shows a warning at the top:
**⚠ N spot breaks on this station's active clock will air NOTHING**, listing each break's minute and category and
what to do (add or activate a spot in that category, or re-pick the category on the clock). The clock screen shows
the same warning under the break (**⚠ 0 eligible spots — this break airs nothing**).

## Good to know

- Spots, sweepers and announcements never enter music rotation.
- Changes to spots and clocks reach the air the next time the log is filled (Fill Day / Fill Week, Chapter 7).
  Making a spot inactive pulls it from the future log at once.
- A spot's play count goes up each time it airs.

# 9. Imaging: sweepers and carts

## What it's for

Imaging is the short produced audio that sounds like your station: legal IDs, stingers, "you're listening to…"
drops, sound effects and beds. Ether handles it two ways:

- **Sweepers** are scheduled. You say which music categories get one, and Ether places a sweeper ahead of every
  song in those categories. The sweeper plays over the end of the song before and into the start of the song it
  introduces. Nothing stops and nothing skips. (Older versions of Ether called some of these "jingles". There is
  now one kind of scheduled imaging, and it is called a sweeper.)
- **Carts** are fired by hand. A cart wall is a grid of buttons; each button holds one piece of audio and plays it
  when you press it.

## Where to find it

- **Bottom bar → SWEEPERS** (next to CATEGORIES): the place where sweepers are set up. It has two tabs:
  **MANAGE** and **ADD IMAGING — CUT A REEL**.
- **☰ menu → Imaging**, or **Tools → Monitors → Imaging**: the IMAGING window, which shows all of your imaging in
  one place. You can also reach it from the SWEEPERS panel with **OPEN IMAGING →**.
- **Bottom bar → CARTS**: the cart wall, pushed up under the decks.
- **☰ menu → Carts**: the cart wall in its own window.

**Plan:** Local (every plan).

---

## Sweepers

### Step 1. Mark your imaging as sweepers

1. Open the **Library**.
2. Right-click a cut and choose **Mark as Sweeper**.
3. The cut now appears in the SWEEPERS panel and in the IMAGING window.

To undo it, right-click the cut again and choose **Unmark Sweeper (→ Music)**.

If your imaging arrived as one long reel, cut it up first: see **Cutting a reel into sweepers** below.

### Step 2. Build pools (recommended)

A pool is a group of sweepers that take turns. Ether plays the one that has gone longest without playing, so the
same cut does not repeat too soon.

1. Open **SWEEPERS** → **MANAGE**.
2. Type a name in **New sweeper pool (e.g. Legal IDs)** and press **Add pool** (or Enter).
3. Click the pool's name in the list. The panel shows **In *pool name*** with the cuts already in it.
4. Under **Add a cut**, type part of a cut's name in the search box. Click a result (marked **+**) to add it.
5. To take a cut out of the pool, press **✕** beside it. The cut stays in the Library and in any other pool.
6. To rename a pool, click its name, type, and click away.
7. To delete a pool, press **✕** at the end of its row and confirm. Categories that used it are left with no
   sweeper; the cuts themselves are not deleted.

Good to know about pools:
- A cut can be in several pools at once. Adding it to one pool takes it out of nothing.
- Every sweeper in the Library is available to every station. Pools are per station.
- Cuts of different lengths can share a pool.
- You can rename a cut from inside the pool: press **EDIT** beside its name. The new name shows everywhere,
  including the Library.

### Step 3. Assign sweepers to categories

This is what decides where sweepers play. Under **Category assignments**, each music category has one row:

| Column | What it does |
|---|---|
| **OVERLAY** | **— none (no sweeper) —**, one **Specific sweeper**, or a **Sweeper pool (rotates)**. |
| **LEAD (s)** | How many seconds before the song starts its sweeper begins. |
| **ACTIVE HOURS** | **Always**, or untick it and pick a first and last hour. |

1. In **OVERLAY**, pick a sweeper or a pool for each category that should get imaging. Leave **— none (no
   sweeper) —** on categories that should not.
2. In **LEAD (s)**, type a number of seconds and press Enter (or click away). A grey number is the station default
   (2 seconds), shown so you can see what is airing. Clear the box to go back to the default.
3. In **ACTIVE HOURS**, untick **Always** to limit imaging to certain hours, then pick the hours.

Every change is saved straight away and applied to the log ahead at once. You do not need to fill the day again.
A line under the assignments says what happened, for example *"KIDS: sweeper saved. Log ahead updated: 212 sweepers
placed (198 replaced)."* If something could not be saved, the line is red and says why.

**None means none.** A category set to none never gets a sweeper. That is a normal programming choice, not an
error.

### Step 4 (optional). AUTO-POST

Under the assignments there is one switch for the whole station: **AUTO-POST OFF / ON**.

- **OFF** (the default): sweepers start LEAD seconds before the next song.
- **ON**: a sweeper from a pool is timed to end exactly where the next song's vocal starts (the intro marker set in
  the Cue Editor). Ether picks a cut from the pool that is short enough.
  - A song with no intro marker uses LEAD instead.
  - A category assigned a single specific sweeper uses LEAD.
  - If no cut in the pool is short enough, that seam gets no sweeper.

To mark a song's intro: right-click the song → **Open in Cue Editor** → **INTRO END**.

---

## Cutting a reel into sweepers (ADD IMAGING — CUT A REEL)

Use this when your imaging arrives as one long file with many cuts separated by silence. It also works for a single
cut you want to import and tag in one go.

1. Open **SWEEPERS** → **ADD IMAGING — CUT A REEL**.
2. Drag the file onto the box that says **Drop a reel — or a single cut — here**, or press **Open reel…**.
3. Ether finds the silent gaps and splits the reel into numbered regions on the waveform.
4. Too many or too few regions? Move the **Silence threshold** slider (−70 to −25 dB) and press **Re-cut**. A
   lower (more negative) number splits on quieter gaps.
5. Check each region:
   - **Space** plays the selected region; press again to stop.
   - **←** / **→** move between regions.
   - **Delete** removes the selected region.
   - Drag the left or right edge of the selected region on the waveform to adjust it.
   - Row buttons: **▶** play · **⌥** split in half · **⌄** merge with the next · **✕** delete.
6. Each region is named after the reel with a number (for example *legal ids 01*). Press **EDIT** on a name to
   change it.
7. Optionally pick a **Pool**.
8. Press **Commit N sweepers →**. Each region is saved as its own file, added to the Library as a sweeper, and put
   in the pool you picked.

When it finishes it says **✓ N of N committed to Library**. If one cut fails, it says which one and how many were
already added; those are really in the Library.

**☰ menu → Imaging** opens one window with five tabs:

- **RACK**: every imaging cut in the Library, with its **Type**, **Length** and the station's **Pools** it is in.

- **POOLS**: the same pool editor as in the SWEEPERS panel. Changes made here are real and saved.
- **ASSIGNMENTS**: the same category assignments as in the SWEEPERS panel, also live.
- **ON DECK**: the next sweepers in the log, each with its time, the cut, and the song it introduces. If nothing is

### Open the cart wall

### Put audio on a button

2. The file is copied into your audio library first, and the button is named after the file.

## Good to know

- **Where carts are heard.** A cart plays on every board channel whose input is set to **Cart / SFX rack**. If no
  channel is, it plays on the built-in cart channel instead. If you press a cart and hear nothing, check that
  channel's fader and ON first.
- Carts are saved per station. Each station has its own wall.
- Sweepers are placed on the songs in the log. With no log ahead there is nothing to place them on — fill the day
  first.
- **LEAD limits:** the box will not go above 90 seconds minus your segue overlap (hover the box to see the exact
  maximum). A LEAD under about 1 second can be missed.
- Sweepers already aired, and sweepers you placed by hand, are never moved when you change an assignment. A sweeper
  already loaded for the very next seam may still play.
- With more than one cart wall in a window, the keys belong to the wall you last clicked.
- **Plan:** carts, sweeper pools and assignments are saved with the station. Having them appear on your other
  computers uses Multi-computer sync, which needs **Live 128, Live 320 or Network**.

# 10. Voice Tracker

## What it's for

The Voice Tracker lets a jock record talk breaks ahead of time and drop each one into the log between two songs,
so it airs at that spot as if the jock were live. You see the song before and the song after while you talk,
record, trim the take, and place it.

## Where to find it

- **☰ → Voice Tracker**: opens it in its own window.

# 11. Announcements and the EAS Logbook

## What it's for

**Announcements** play recorded messages at set times on calendar dates — "the park closes in 30 minutes", "the
parade starts at 7", a legal notice. The programme ducks under the announcement and comes back, even in the middle
of a song.

The **EAS Logbook** is your Emergency Alert System record: every required weekly and monthly test and every real
alert your station received or sent, with a compliance summary and printouts for an inspector. Ether keeps the log;
it does not generate EAS tones (that is your encoder/decoder's job).

## Where to find it

- **Schedule → Announcements**, or **☰ menu → Announcements**.
- **Schedule → EAS Logbook**, or **☰ menu → EAS Logbook**. Both open it in its own window.

**Plan:** Local (every plan).

## Announcements

### Before anything can air: patch an Announcement channel

An announcement plays on a board channel whose input is set to **Announcement**. If there is none, the top of the
page says so in amber, and the **▶ AIR** buttons are greyed.

1. On the board, press **+** and choose **Announcement**.
2. Press that channel's **DUCK** button (lit amber) so the programme steps back while it plays.

How far the programme ducks, and for how long, is set per station in **Settings → Ducker**. There is no
per-announcement duck setting.

### Add an announcement (the audio)

An announcement is just a name and an audio file. When it plays is set separately, on the calendar.

1. Press **＋ Add Announcement** (top right).
2. Pick the audio file. It is copied into your audio library.
3. Check the **Title** and press **Save**.

In the list at the bottom of the page each announcement shows:
- **Scheduled**: the next date it plays and when. **not scheduled** (amber) means it will never fire — the first
  thing to check if something didn't go to air.
- **Status**: **ON** / **OFF**. Click to switch. An announcement that is OFF does not fire.
- **▶ AIR**: play it on air now, on the Announcement channel.
- **▶ Test**: hear it on this computer only. It does not go to air.
- **Edit**: change the title.
- **✕**: delete it. Ether asks first in its own confirm window, and tells you how many schedule lines go with it.
  Press **OK** to delete, **Cancel** (or Esc) to keep it.

### Schedule it: Select → Edit → Apply

Scheduling works on real calendar dates. There is no "every Wednesday": you pick the actual dates. **Nothing is
written until you press APPLY.**

**One date:**
1. Click the date in the **Dates** calendar. The **Schedule** column loads what is on that day.
2. Change what you want (see *Building the list* below).
3. Press **APPLY to 1 date**. Only that day changes, and the panel clears.

**Several dates at once:**
1. Click each date you want. The first click loads that date's list; later clicks just add dates to the selection.
   - Click a weekday letter at the top of the calendar to select every one of those days in the month shown.
   - Use **‹** and **›** to change month. The selection stays as you move between months.
   - **Clear *n*** removes the whole selection.
2. Build the list.
3. Press **APPLY to *n* dates**. Every selected date gets exactly that list.

**Copy one day to others:** click the day to copy (it loads), click the other dates, then press Apply.

### Building the list

Press **＋ Add Announcement** in the Schedule column to add a line. On each line:

1. Pick the announcement.
2. Pick how it is timed:
   - **at a set time**: type the time.
   - **before closing**: type the minutes (and, if you want, seconds) before closing. A negative number is before
     closing; 0 is closing time itself.
3. Pick how many times it plays in a row (**×1** to **×10**). Each repeat starts when the one before ends.
4. The time at the end of the line is when it will actually fire. **no time** (red) means it cannot play yet.
5. **✕** removes the line.

If any line is timed from closing, a box appears above the list: **This date closes at** (or **These *n* dates
close at**). Set the closing time there. It is saved with the lines when you press Apply, for the selected dates
only. If no closing time is set, the panel says the closing-time lines cannot play.

If the same announcement is on the list twice, the panel warns you that it will play more than once.

### What Apply does

- Apply makes each selected date's list **exactly** what is in the editor. Anything on those dates that is not in
  the editor is removed.
- If any selected date already has announcements, you are asked to confirm with **Replace** or **Cancel**.
- Applying an empty list clears those dates. The panel warns you first.
- **unapplied changes** (amber) next to the button means you have edited but not applied. **Cancel** throws the
  edits away.
- A date on the calendar showing **♪3** has three announcements on it.

### TONIGHT: did it play?

At the top of the page, **TONIGHT** lists today's announcements in the order they play.

- While one is on air, its line pulses red with **ON AIR** and the seconds left.
- Afterwards it says **Played *time*** (green).
- **Failed *time*: reason** (red): Ether tried to play it and could not; the reason says why.
- **Skipped *time*** (amber): the closing time moved and its time had already passed.
- **Did not play** (red): its time has passed (plus the late limit) and nothing says it played.
- **No time — no closing time set, so it will not play** (amber).
- **Up next** / **Waiting** / **Due now**: not played yet.

**Late limit.** At the top of TONIGHT: *Late limit: a line may still start up to [ ] seconds after its time.* If Ether
is busy at the exact second, a line may still start within this many seconds. Type whole seconds (0 to 3600) and
press **Save**. Leave it empty and press Save to clear it: lines then fire only on their own second.

# 14. Streaming

## What it's for

This chapter covers how your station gets from the board to your listeners. Ether sends the station's programme to
an Icecast server, so anyone with the listener link can tune in. You start and stop that stream with the **ON AIR**
button or from **Stream Manager**. Ether can also send the title of each song to your stream, TuneIn, an RDS
encoder or a webhook (**Stream Metadata Outputs**). Before it reaches the stream, the programme can go through the
**Processor**. That is a loudness ride and a true-peak limiter, with a master EQ in front of them. This chapter also covers where your own speakers, headphones
(PFL) and the aux play on this computer (the **Patch Bay** and **Master Out**). Last, it covers the sound settings
that apply to every source.

## Where to find it

- **ON AIR** button: top bar, just left of the ☰ menu button.
- Stream settings and status: **File → Settings → Broadcast**.
- Stream Manager: **☰ → Stream Manager**, or **Tools → Stream Manager**.
- Song titles to your stream and other outputs: **File → Settings → Broadcast → Stream Metadata Outputs**.
- Server address, mount, password and bitrate for each station: **Tools → Station Manager**, then **Rename** on the
  station, or **Stream Manager** (the same settings).
- Processor: **Master Out → Processor → OPEN**, or **☰ → Processor**, or **Tools → Monitors → Processor**.
- Patch Bay: **☰ → Patch Bay**, or **Tools → Patch Bay**.
- Master Out: the panel on the right of the main screen. **Tools → Monitors → Master Output** opens it in its own window.
- Sound settings: **File → Settings → Audio** and **File → Settings → Audio Devices**.

**Plan:**
- Streaming to **your own Icecast server**: Local (every plan).
- Streaming to **Ether's hosted stream**: Live 128, Live 320 or Network.
- Stream Manager and Stream Metadata Outputs run on this computer: Local (every plan). The **Listen live** link in
  Stream Manager is your public listener page: Local (every plan).
- The Processor, Patch Bay, Master Out and the Audio settings run on this computer alone: Local (every plan).

---

## Set up where the stream goes

Each station streams to its own Icecast mount. You set these details once per station.

1. Open **Tools → Station Manager**.
2. On your station's card, press **Rename**. The edit form opens.
3. Under **ICECAST**, fill in:
   - **Server URL**: the address of your Icecast server, for example `stream.example.com` or an IP address. If
     your server does not use port 8000, write the port after the address, for example `stream.example.com:8443`.
     With no port written, Ether uses 8000.
   - **Mount**: the mount point, for example `/live`.
   - **Password**: the Icecast source password.
   - **Bitrate**: 64 to 320 kbps.
4. Press **Save Changes**.

If you leave **Server URL** blank, Ether streams to Ether's hosted server. If you leave **Password** blank, Ether
uses `hackme`. If you leave **Mount** blank, Ether uses `/live`.

You can also change the server for the station you are working on from **Settings → Broadcast**. Next to the server
address at the top, press **Edit**, type the new address and press **Save** (or Enter). Press **✕** or Esc to cancel.

## Stream Manager

Stream Manager is the full view of the stream for the station you are working on. It is the **same stream as the ON
AIR button**. Starting or stopping it here does exactly what ON AIR does, and the two always agree.

1. Open **☰ → Stream Manager** (or **Tools → Stream Manager**). It opens in its own window and shows the station you
   are working on.
2. Read the status at the top:
   - **OFF AIR**, **CONNECTING…**, **ON AIR** or **ERROR**. While on air, it shows how long, for example
     *"on air 1m 14s"*.
   - **Sending to**: the server, port and mount the stream goes to.
   - **Bitrate**: while on air, what the encoder is really sending. Otherwise the station's setting, marked **(set)**.
   - **Listeners**: how many people are listening to this mount right now. Ether reads this from your Icecast
     server's own status page, every 15 seconds while on air.
3. Under **Listen live** is your station's public listener page. Press **Copy** to copy the address, or **Open** to
   open it in your browser. If the station has no listener page yet, it says to turn one on in **Settings →
   Broadcast → Your listener page**.
4. To change where the stream goes, edit the fields under **Icecast server for this station**: the server, the
   mount, the password and the bitrate (64 to 320 kbps). Leave the password blank to keep the one already set. Press
   **Save settings**. These are the same settings as Station Manager; there is only one set.
5. Press **▶ Start streaming** to start the stream. Press **⏹ Stop streaming** to stop. The ON AIR button on the main screen
   changes with it.

- Saved settings are used the next time the stream starts. If you save while on air, Ether says so. Stop and start
  the stream to switch over.
- If the listener count cannot be read (the server does not answer, or its status page is off), the box shows
  **unknown** and the reason. Ether never guesses. **mount not on server** means the server has no stream on that
  mount right now.
- If the stream keeps failing, the status shows **ERROR** with the server's own reason. For example: *"The server
  refused the stream (403): another computer may be sending to this mount, or the password is wrong. Ether tries
  again every 30 seconds; press Stop to end it."* **⏹ Stop streaming** stays on screen while Ether retries.

## Start streaming

**Start streaming** sends your station to your stream server, so listeners can hear it. The quickest place is
**☰ → Stream Manager** (above) or the **OFF AIR** button in the top bar; both do the same thing.

**From the top bar:**

1. Press **OFF AIR**.
2. The button turns amber and reads **GOING LIVE…** while Ether connects to the server.
3. When the server accepts the stream, the button turns red and reads **ON AIR**.
4. To stop, press the button again. It goes back to **OFF AIR**.

The button only shows the station you are working on. If you switch to another station, the button shows that
station's stream. Another station can stay on air while you build a different one.

**From Settings → Broadcast:**

1. Open **File → Settings → Broadcast**. The **Broadcast** section lists every station on this computer.
2. Each station shows **ONLINE** or **OFFLINE**, and its **Listener URL**. Press **Copy** to copy the listener link.
3. Press **▶ GO LIVE — Stream to Icecast** to start that station's stream.
4. Press **■ STOP STREAM** to stop it.

A status line under each station shows **CONNECTING…**, **LIVE** (with how long it has been live) or **ERROR**
(with the message). A green or red line under the list shows the result of your last start, for example
*"✓ Streaming → server:8000/live"*.

The dot next to the server address at the top shows whether that server answers: **ONLINE**, **OFFLINE** or
**CHECKING**. Press **Refresh** to check again.

## Stream Metadata Outputs (song titles)

Every time a song starts on a station, Ether sends **"Artist - Title"** to each output you add here, all at once. Use
it to show the song title on your stream, in TuneIn, on an FM radio's display (RDS), or in your own tools. Outputs
belong to the station you are working on and are kept on this computer. Ether sends them in the background, so titles
keep going out with no window open.

### Kinds of output

- **Icecast 2**: the song title on an Icecast mount. Tick **This station's own stream (server, mount and password
  from Stream Manager)** to use the stream ON AIR sends. Untick it to type another server's **Server host**,
  **Port**, **Mount point**, **Admin username**, **Admin password** and **Use HTTPS**.
- **Shoutcast**: a Shoutcast server. Fill in **Server host**, **Port**, **Stream ID (sid)**, **Version** (v1 or v2)
  and **Admin password**.
- **TuneIn AIR**: your **Partner ID**, **Partner Key** and **Station ID**. You get these from tunein.com/broadcasters.
- **RDS Encoder (Serial)**: an FM RDS encoder on a serial (COM) port. Pick the **Serial port** (the screen lists the
  ports this computer has), the **Baud rate** and the **Encoder type**: Inovonics, Audemat / WorldCast, Deva, or
  Generic / plain text. The text is cut to 64 characters.
- **Custom Webhook**: Ether sends the song as JSON (title, artist, album, duration, time) to your **Webhook URL**, by
  POST or PUT, with an optional user name and password.

### Add an output

1. Open **File → Settings → Broadcast** and go to **Stream Metadata Outputs**. It shows the station you are working
   on.
2. Under **+ ADD A NEW METADATA OUTPUT**, press the kind you want.
3. Give it a **Name** and fill in the fields. Leave **Enabled** ticked.
4. Press **Save**.
5. Press **Test** on its row. Ether sends the station's current song to that output now. The row says what was sent
   (*"✓ Sent …"*) or why it failed.

### Manage outputs

- **ON / OFF** pauses an output or turns it back on.
- **Edit** changes it. **Del** deletes it after you confirm.
- Each row shows its last result (**OK**, or **ERROR** with the reason, for example *"HTTP 401"*), how many titles
  it has sent, when it last sent, and the last title it sent.

Good to know for outputs:

- A slow or failing output never holds up the others.
- **Test** sends *"Ether - Test from Ether"* when nothing has played on the station since Ether started.
- Outputs are not copied to your other computers. A serial port and a server password belong to the computer that
  sends.
- An Icecast output can only set a title while that mount has a stream on it. With nothing streaming, the server
  answers with an error.

## The Processor (loudness ride and limiter)

The Processor shapes the station's sound after the mix, in this order:

- **PGM**: the **GEQ**, a 10-band master EQ for the whole programme.
- **MONITOR** (your local output): the **RIDE** (loudness ride), then the **LIMITER** (true-peak limiter).
- **STREAM** (what listeners get): its own **RIDE**, then **LIMITER**.

### Turn processing on

Processing is **off** by default. When it is off, the sound passes through untouched.

1. Open **File → Settings → Broadcast**, then go to **Audio Processing**.
2. Turn on **Process stream** to process what listeners hear.
3. Turn on **Process local output** to process this computer's speakers. This does not change the stream.
4. With either one on, **Live meters** appear. Below them, a line says what is running, for example *"Riding to
   −14 LUFS · limiter holds −1.0 dBTP · stream only."*

Each station has its own settings. A change reaches the audio engine within a few seconds.

### Adjust the chain

1. Open the Processor: **Master Out → Processor → OPEN**. It opens in its own window.
   **☰ → Processor** opens the same window. The **Channel** selector at the top lists every deck and fader on the board,
   then **MASTER OUT**; pick one to adjust its EQ. This window is the only place EQ is set.
2. Tap a module in the strip. The editor below names it, for example *"editing: MONITOR · RIDE"*.
3. Change the controls. Each change goes to air at once and is saved for this station.
   - **GEQ**: ten band faders over a live spectrum, **FLAT** (all bands to 0 dB), and **IN / OUT**.
   - **RIDE**: Target (the loudness it aims for), Rate (how fast it moves), Clamp (how far it may go).
   - **LIMITER**: Ceiling and Release.
4. **LINKED ⇄** means the stream runs the same settings as the monitor. Press it to **SPLIT**. The stream then gets
   its own settings, starting as a copy of the monitor. Press **SPLIT** again to link them back.

The meters on the right stay in view: **IN · PGM**, **OUT · MON** and **OUT · STR**. Below them are the ride and
limiter meters and the loudness panels for **Monitor** and **Stream**.

### Presets

1. Pick a preset from **Arm a preset…**. Nothing changes on air yet.
2. A yellow line shows what **TAKE** will change.
3. Press **TAKE ▸ (preset name)** to apply it, or **DISARM** to drop it.

- **SAVE** overwrites your own active preset. You cannot overwrite a built-in preset.
- To make a new preset, type a name in **Save as…** and press **SAVE AS**.
- **· modified** next to the preset name means the rack no longer matches that preset.
- A preset never turns processing on or off. That stays with the two switches in Settings → Broadcast.

### Bypass (testing only)

**BYP** on the ride or the limiter takes that module out of the chain straight away. A yellow banner warns you, for
example *"STREAM: LIMITER BYPASSED — nothing is holding the ceiling on air"*. Bypass is never saved. It clears when
Ether restarts. Press **BYP** again to put the module back **IN**. Master Out also shows **BYPASSED** next to
**Processor** while a bypass is on.

## Master Out

Master Out is the panel on the right of the main screen.

- **Bus meters** and **LIM** (how hard the limiter is working).
- **Master** fader: the station's programme level.
- **Monitor** fader: your local listening level.
- **Limiter**: the limiter's live reading.
- **Processor → OPEN**: opens the Processor (see above).
- **Station** (click to open): **On Air** shows **LIVE** while programme audio is playing, and **STBY** when it is
  not. **Stream** shows the station's real stream state: **ON AIR**, **CONNECTING**, **ERROR** or **OFF** (the same
  as Stream Manager). **Uptime** and **Next Break** are shown too.
- **Station monitors**: one strip for each station on this computer.
  - **MUTE** silences that station's local speakers and keeps the level. Press **MUTED** to bring the sound back.
  - Open a strip to get its **Monitor** fader (local speakers only) and the **Park page control** switch (see
    chapter 16).
  - A line shows which device the station's Master really plays to, and opens the Patch Bay.
- The arrow at the top left collapses the panel to a thin meter strip. The pop-out icon opens Master Out in its own
  window.

Monitor levels are what you hear in the room. They never change the stream.

## The Patch Bay (where this computer plays)

The Patch Bay is the one place where you choose which device each output plays to. Its settings are saved for this
computer only.

### Outputs

Each station has three rows:
- **Master**: the station's local output, after the MONITOR fader. If nothing is patched, it plays on the Windows
  default device.
- **Cue (PFL)**: where PFL plays. If nothing is patched, PFL plays over Master.
- **Aux**: the aux monitor bus. If nothing is patched, the aux is silent.

The columns are every channel pair on every output device (for example **1-2**, **3-4**).

1. Open **☰ → Patch Bay**.
2. Click a square to connect that output to that device's channels. Click it again to disconnect.
3. One output can play to up to 4 devices at once.
4. The lines under the grid say what the engine really has open. For example: **Live** with the device name, or
   **MISSING** in red if the device is not connected. Ether never plays somewhere else in its place.
5. Press **Refresh output devices** after you plug in a new device.

**If the Master device stalls, switch to the Windows default:** choose **On**, **Off** or **Not set**. When it is
**Not set**, a stalling device is shown in red and retried. Ether never switches on its own.

### Inputs

Under each station's outputs is an **Inputs** grid. Its rows are the station's source faders. Its columns are every
input on this computer, the Park mics, and the Remote Link feed.

1. Click a square to bring that input in on that fader. The fader's SOURCE on the board changes with it.
2. Click it again to disconnect.
3. The lines under the grid say what each fader is receiving.
4. Press **Refresh inputs** after you plug in a new device.

If the station has no source faders, the grid says so. Add one with **+** on the board first.

## Settings → Audio Devices

- **Where each station plays**: this is a pointer to the Patch Bay (see above).
- **Phone hybrid input**: the input your phone hybrid or caller audio is connected to. The Phone Desk uses it. The
  chosen input shows **ACTIVE**.
- **Track Editor preview player**: where the Track Editor plays when you audition a cut. It is not the PFL. The
  chosen output shows **PREVIEW**.
- **↻ Rescan Devices**: looks for devices again.
- **Segue overlap (auto)**: how many seconds the next song starts before the current one ends, 0 to 10. **off** means
  wait for the end. The outgoing song always plays to its natural end. This is saved with the station.

## Settings → Audio

### Loudness & faders

- **Loudness target**: every file on any fader (songs, sweepers, carts, announcements, the jukebox) plays at this
  loudness. Ether measures each file once and sets its gain to match. The range is −40 to −5 LUFS. Type a value and
  press **Save**. **now:** shows the stored value. A new target applies to each file from its next load. The line
  below shows how many files have been measured and how many are waiting.
- **Fader smoothing**: how long every level move takes, so a move never clicks. The range is 0 to 500 ms.
- **Fader maximum (top of travel)**: the top level for **Channel faders**, **AUX rows**, **MASTER** and **MONITOR**,
  from 0 to +10 dB.
- **How a fader moves**: Shift-drag fine factor, mouse wheel step, arrow key step and Shift + arrow step.
- The fader law points: **Add a point**, **Remove**, then **Save law**.

### PFL (pre-fader listen)

- PFL plays a channel in this station's local output, or in the **PFL output** if you patched one (the Cue row in the
  Patch Bay).
- **Programme dip**: how far the programme drops in your local output while any PFL is on. 0 dB means no dip.
  −60 dB means almost gone. Air and the stream are never affected.
- The line below says whether the value is saved for this station.

### Mic Inputs (on air)

- Each mic channel on the board is listed with the engine's live state.
- The input itself is chosen in the Patch Bay. Press **Patch Bay** to go there. Press **Disconnect** to remove the
  input; the channel stays set to Mic.
- **Input gain**: digital gain from −10 to +40 dB. Set the mic's preamp first, then trim here.
- To hear yourself, use your interface's direct monitor. The engine's path back to your headphones is too late to
  talk against.

### Ducker

The Ducker sets how far the programme drops when a source channel with **DUCK** lit has audio.

- **Depth** (dB down), **Hold**, **Release**, **Attack** and **Threshold**. Each control has a hint under it.
- **Which decks step back**: ticked decks duck under a source. Unticked decks play through at full level.
- **Reset to the starting values** puts the starting values back. If the station has no ducker settings yet, the
  panel says the music is not ducked and offers **Use the starting values**.

These settings are for the station you are working on.

## Good to know

- **The stream starts from the programme, not from your speakers.** Patch Bay devices, the MONITOR fader and the
  station monitor strips never change what listeners hear. If no Master device is open, the Patch Bay says the
  station runs on its own clock and the stream keeps going.
- **The stream goes to the port written in the server address**, for example `stream.example.com:8443`. With no
  port written, it goes to port 8000. The format is always MP3. The **Format** choice in Station Manager does not
  change the stream.
- **Settings → Broadcast always shows port 8000** in its result line (*"✓ Streaming → server:8000/live"*) and checks
  the server's ONLINE dot and builds each station's **Listener URL** with port 8000, even when you wrote another
  port. Stream Manager's **Sending to** shows the real port.
- **Edit in Settings → Broadcast changes only the station you are working on**, even though the server appears
  above the whole list.
- **If the stream fails three times within 10 seconds**, Ether shows **ERROR** with the server's reason, then
  *"Ether tries again every 30 seconds; press Stop to end it."* It keeps trying until you stop it.
- **After an update, fully close and reopen Ether.** The audio engine does not reload on its own. Several screens say
  this if the running engine is too old. For example, the Processor may say the limiter ceiling is "not reported".
- **Once you have changed anything in the Processor window, set the loudness target there.** After that, the
  **Target loudness** box in Settings → Broadcast → Audio Processing no longer changes what runs.
- **A red line in the Processor** (*"The engine did not accept that"*) means nothing changed on air. The rack shows
  what is really running.

# 15. Remote Link

## What it's for

The Remote Link carries a live feed from one Ether computer to a fader on another, in about a fifth of a second. Use it for a remote broadcast: the computer at the venue sends its board to the studio, and at the studio the feed lands on a fader whose source is **Link**. From there it is a channel like any other — its meter, fader, ON button, the ducker, and the station's processing. The listener stream is about 8 seconds behind, far too late to cue from; the Link is not.

The computer that **sends** is the one that owns the key. The computer that **receives** picks the sending computer on a fader set to Link.

## Where to find it

- ☰ menu → **Remote Link** (the button's tooltip reads "Take a remote broadcast from another Ether computer, or send this station to another (SEND TO)"). It opens Settings on the Remote Link page.
- **Settings → Remote Link**.
- On the board: any source fader's source menu → **Link (network feed)**.

The page has three boxes: **This computer's link key**, **Feed in — the fader set to Link**, and **SEND FEED TO another computer**.

**Plan:**
- Sending to a typed address, and pasting a key line by hand: Local (every plan).
- Picking a computer on your account, or pairing a guest computer with a code: Live 128, Live 320 or Network.
- Sending through Ether's relay (no address to type): Live 128, Live 320 or Network.

## Receive a remote feed (the studio side)

1. On the board, pick a source fader. Open its source menu and choose **Link (network feed)**.
2. The strip now shows **● LINK** and a list: **— pick the sending computer —**. It lists the other computers on your Ether account.
3. Pick the computer that will send. Its key is fetched for you; there is nothing to copy.
   - A computer shows **not shared yet — open Ether on it** until Ether has been opened on it. It cannot be picked until then.
4. The fader now shows the sender's name and key fingerprint, e.g. **← VENUE-PC · key …**. Compare that fingerprint with the one on the sending computer's screen (Settings → Remote Link → **Key**).
5. When the sending computer starts its feed, the fader carries it. Bring the fader up and turn the channel ON as you would any source.

You can do the same from **Settings → Remote Link → Feed in**: the **Takes the feed of** row shows the same picker.

**From a computer on a different Ether account (a guest):**

1. On the sending computer, go to Settings → Remote Link → **This computer's link key** and press **Show a pairing code**. An 8-character code appears with a countdown.
2. On the receiving computer, type the code into **guest code XXXX-XXXX** (on the strip or in **Takes the feed of**) and press **Pair** (or Enter).
3. The code works once and expires after 10 minutes. Press **New pairing code** for another.

**The advanced way (paste a key line):**

1. On the sending computer: **Key** → **Advanced — copy the key as a line** → **Copy link key line**. The line is copied (if the copy fails, select the line shown and copy it).
2. On the receiving computer: **Advanced — paste a link key line** → paste it. It is applied when you paste or press Enter. The line starts with `ether-link:1:`.

### Feed in settings

These appear once a fader is set to Link. Each one is applied when you leave the box.

- **Jitter buffer** — how much audio the Link holds to ride out network wobble, in milliseconds. More means fewer dropouts and more delay. The allowed range and the default are shown beside the box.
- **Listen on UDP port** — the port this computer listens on, for every station on it. If a router forwards the remote's traffic to this computer, it points at this port. The default and the port in use are shown.
- **Auto-cut on loss** — **ON** / **OFF**, and **turn the channel OFF after … s of loss**. When ON, if the Link is lost for that long the channel is turned OFF, so the remote never comes back to air unannounced. It is OFF to start with: the channel just goes silent.

The coloured line next to **Feed in** says what the Link is doing right now. Below the settings, a line of live numbers shows latency (network + buffered), jitter, kb/s, packets received and lost, dropouts, refused keys and reconnects.

### Allowing a computer the first time (relay)

When a computer sends to this station through Ether's relay for the first time, and the Link fader does not already take its feed, an amber box appears at the top of Settings → Remote Link: "**VENUE-PC wants to send a feed to [station]'s Link fader. Allow it?**" Press **Allow**. It is asked once and remembered after.

### Cutting a sender off

- On the strip, press **✕** next to the sender's name, or
- In Settings → Remote Link, press **Forget key — cut [name] off**.

That fader no longer takes that computer's feed until it is paired again.

## Send this station to another computer (SEND TO)

1. Go to Settings → Remote Link → **SEND FEED TO another computer**.
2. In **Computer · station**, pick where the feed goes. The list shows each station on your account and the computer that airs it. A line that cannot be used is greyed and says why.
   - If the list is empty: "No computer airs a station on this account yet — a station's computer is set when it takes over playout."
3. Leave the address empty to send through **Ether's relay**: no address or port to type, and no router setup at either end. The first time, the receiving computer is asked to allow this one (see above).
4. To send **directly** instead, open **Advanced: send directly to an address instead** and type that computer's public address and **port**. Its router must forward that port to it.
5. Pick a **Bitrate** (64 to 256 kb/s; the default is marked). The audio is Opus, stereo, 48 kHz.
6. **Loss protection (FEC)** — **ON** / **OFF**. With it ON and nothing lost, the audio is full quality. When the far end reports loss, part of the audio is coded as speech to carry a spare copy, which is less clean on music.
7. Press **▶ SEND FEED TO [computer · station]**. The line next to the heading turns to **SENDING …**.
8. To stop, press **■ STOP THE FEED**.

What is sent is this station's programme **before** its processing, so the board it lands on processes it once. Sending the same station from the venue to the studio is the normal case. While sending, the settings above are locked, and a line of live numbers shows round-trip time, frames sent, send errors, and what the receiving computer reports back (received, lost, buffer, jitter).

## This computer's link key

- **Key** shows this computer's key fingerprint, its number and when it was made. If there is no key yet, one is made the first time it is needed.
- **Replace key…** → **Replace — every fader holding the old key stops taking this feed** makes a new key. Press **Keep** to back out. Faders on your own account that picked this computer from the list pick up the new key within about a minute. Faders paired with a code or a pasted line stop taking this computer's feed until they are paired again.

## Good to know

- The Remote Link needs Ether's audio engine service. If it is not running, the page says: "The Remote Link needs the audio engine service — fully close and reopen Ether."
- The Link goes on a source fader only.
- A computer cannot feed its own faders; that line is refused in the **Computer · station** list.
- The receiving fader takes one sender at a time. A second computer trying to send to the same fader is refused and counted ("second sender refused").
- Only the computer whose key the fader holds is accepted; a wrong key is refused and counted ("key refused").
- The relay works between computers on the **same** Ether account. For a guest computer on another account, use a typed address.
- A pairing code works once, for 10 minutes.
- The **SRT → UDP fallback** row is shown but cannot be changed: this build sends UDP only.
- A Link coming up, being lost, and a key being refused are recorded in the Health Monitor.

# 16. The Park page

## What it's for

The Park page is a web page for the people walking the park, on their own phone. It shows the time, what is on air,
any announcement playing right now, tonight's closing time and today's announcements. Staff who log in can also
change tonight's closing time, turn the park's sound up or down, MUTE it, fire carts 1 to 8, listen to what the park
speakers are playing, and talk over the park speakers. Nothing needs to be installed: it opens in an ordinary phone
browser. The page is hosted on the internet, so the address keeps working even when the studio computer is off; it
then says the station isn't reporting.

## Where to find it

- **The page's address:** in Ether, **Settings → Links → Park page**. There is one line per station, with **Open** and
  **Copy**. The address is `park.ether-cast.com/` followed by the station's public page name.
- **Its settings:** **Settings → Park Page** (two sections: **Park page — Listen** and **Park page — Hold to talk**).
- **The studio's lock and the park's sound in Ether:** **Monitors → Master Output → Station Monitors**, on the
  station's strip.

**Plan:** Live 128, Live 320 or Network

## Get the address to your staff

1. Open **Settings → Links** and pick **Park page**.
2. Find the line for your station. If it says *"this station has no public page address yet"*, the station needs a
   public page address first.
3. Press **Copy** and text or post the address. Press **Open** to see the page yourself.

Anyone with the address can look at the page. To make changes, a person must log in (see below).

## Who can log in

Staff log in on the Park page with a **username** and **PIN**. These are your Ether users: a user added in
**Settings → Station → Users & Security** with a PIN can log in to the Park page with that name and PIN (a user without
a PIN cannot). The same users work on every station of the account.

- If someone's PIN is changed or the user is removed, their phone is logged out the next time the page checks.
- Too many wrong PINs locks that user out for a while; the page says when they can try again.

## Use the Park page (on the phone)

1. Open the address. Without logging in, the page is view only and says *"View only. Log in to make changes."*
2. Press **LOG IN**, type your **Username** and **PIN**, and press **LOG IN**. The page now shows
   *"Logged in: <your name>"*. It stays logged in on that phone until you press **LOG OUT**.

The page has three columns.

**Left — what is happening**
- The clock.
- **ANNOUNCEMENT ON AIR** appears while an announcement is playing, with its name and the seconds left.
- **Now playing** — the song and the time left. *"Station is off air"* or *"Nothing playing"* when there is nothing.
- **Park closes tonight** — tonight's closing time.
- **Announcements today** — every announcement for today, in the order it will play, with its time. Lines timed from
  closing say so (for example *"20 min before closing"*). Each line can show **ON AIR NOW**, **Played**, **Failed**,
  **Skipped** or **Did not play**.

**Middle — Park sound**
- One big fader and a **MUTE** button. The fader is the station's speaker level in the studio (what the park hears).
- Under it, a line says who last changed the sound and when, for example *"Muted from Park page by Ryan, 8:42 PM"*.
- Logged-in staff also see **LISTEN** and **TAP TO TALK** here.

**Right — Carts**
- Eight buttons, **1** to **8**: the first eight carts on the station's cart wall. Empty ones say **Empty**.

## Change tonight's closing time

1. Log in.
2. Tap the time under **Park closes tonight**.
3. Set the new time and press **SAVE** (or **Cancel** to keep the old one).
4. The page shows *"Saved — waiting for the station to confirm…"*, then *"Saved. The announcements below have moved
   with it."* Announcements timed from closing move with the new time.

This changes **tonight only**. Other days, and the weekday closing times, are not touched.

## Turn the park's sound up, down or off

1. Log in.
2. Drag the fader. It moves from where it is — touching it does not make it jump. You can also double-tap the level
   readout to type an exact level in dB. On a computer, the mouse wheel and arrow keys also move it.
3. Tap **MUTE** to silence the park. It turns to **MUTED**. Tap it again to bring the sound back at the level it had.

Any logged-in person can change or undo anyone's change. Ether shows the same "who and when" line on the station's
strip.

## Fire a cart

1. Log in.
2. Tap a cart button. While it plays, the button shows the time left.
3. Tap the same button again to stop it.

## Listen to the park on your phone

Listen plays, on your own phone only, everything the park hears from the studio: every MAIN and AUX source (music,
announcements, commercials, sound effects, carts), after the station's speaker level and MUTE. Your phone's volume
never changes the park.

**Set it up first, in Ether (Settings → Park Page → Park page — Listen):**
1. **Listen quality** — a number from 6 to 510 kbps. Press **Save**.
2. **Channels** — **Mono** or **Stereo**. Press **Save**.
3. **Frame length** — 2.5, 5, 10, 20, 40 or 60 ms. Shorter means less delay. Press **Save**.
4. **Listener buffer (on the phone)** — 0 to 4000 ms. More means fewer dropouts on a weak connection, but more delay
   (0 = the phone's own minimum). Press **Save**.

Each row shows **now:** and the stored value. Listen stays off for the station until all four are set; the panel says
*"Listen is available on this station's Park page."* once they are. A new setting applies the next time someone presses
Listen.

**On the phone:**
1. Log in and tap **LISTEN**. It turns to **LISTENING**.
2. Tap it again to stop.

If the station is not set up, the button says *"Listen isn't set up on this station"*. Nothing is sent unless someone
is listening, and Listen never touches the on-air stream or playout.

## Talk over the park speakers (TAP TO TALK)

There are two Park mics, **Park Mic 1** and **Park Mic 2**. Each is an input like a sound-card mic.

**Set it up first, in Ether:**
1. On the board, set a fader's source to **Mic** and pick **Park Mic 1 (Park page)** (or Park Mic 2). Put each Park mic
   on one fader only.
2. The fader's **MAIN** button decides where the voice goes: lit = in the park **and** on air; not lit = in the park
   only. **DUCK** dips the music while someone talks. Any processing (EQ, compressor) goes in that fader's own rack.
3. Open **Settings → Park Page → Park page — Hold to talk**. For each Park mic, type the **name on the phone** (up to 40
   characters) and press **Save**. Under each mic the panel shows which fader it is on and whether MAIN is lit.
4. Set **Park mic buffer** (20 to 1000 ms; more = a steadier voice on a weak signal, a little more delay) and press
   **Save**.
5. Click **Phone echo cancellation**, **Phone noise suppression** and **Phone automatic gain** to set each ON or OFF.
   Talk stays off until these three are set.

**On the phone:**
1. Log in. If there are two Park mics, pick one by its name.
2. Tap **TAP TO TALK**. The first time, the phone asks for permission to use the microphone — allow it.
3. Wait for green **TALK NOW**, then talk. (**CONNECTING…** means not yet.)
4. Tap again to stop. There is no time limit.
5. When you are done for a while, tap **Turn the microphone off**.

If the line drops during a talk, the button says **DISCONNECTED** and stays on until you tap it. Only one person can
talk on a mic at a time; the other phones show who is talking. While you talk, Listen on your own phone goes quiet so
the phone does not feed back into itself.

**In Ether:** while someone talks, the Park mic's fader shows *"Talking now: <name> (Park page)"* and a **STOP TALK**
button that ends it. Afterwards it shows who talked last, when, and for how long.

## Lock the Park page from the studio

1. Open **Monitors → Master Output**. Under **Station Monitors**, find the station's strip.
2. Set **Park page control** to **OFF**.

While it is OFF, every Park page shows *"Controls locked by the studio"*: the fader, MUTE, carts, closing time and Tap to
Talk are locked. Listen still works. Only Ether can set it back to **ON**.

## Good to know

- **The studio computer must be on and connected for changes.** The closing time, sound, MUTE, carts and talk are sent
  to the computer that is playing the station. If that computer is offline, the change is not sent and the phone says
  so; it is not held for later.
- **Carts fire on the station that is open on screen in Ether.** If the station is not the one showing on that
  computer, the cart does not fire.
- **What the page shows is what the station reports.** After a change, the page shows the new value once the station
  confirms it (usually within a few seconds).
- *"This station isn't reporting."* — the page loaded, but the studio computer isn't sending anything. It fills in once
  that computer is running Ether.
- *"Can't reach the station right now."* — the phone couldn't reach the page's server. It keeps trying and shows the
  last update.
- *"No park at this address."* — the address doesn't match a station's public page. Check the link.
- Microphone blocked: the page shows the steps for iPhone or Android to allow the microphone, then reload.
- The Park page cannot start, stop or change the music log, and cannot take the station off air.

# 17. Jukebox

## What it's for

The Jukebox is a public request wall. It opens as its own window that you put on a screen the public can see. It shows a wall of album covers. A guest taps a cover, types their name, and the song joins a queue on the right of the screen with their name and their place in line. Guests can also scan a QR code and request from their phone. The jukebox plays through a source channel on your board, like a microphone: it is on air only when that channel is on and its fader is up. It never adds anything to your station's log, never changes your clocks or rotation, and never touches your station's AUTO/MANUAL. It plays music only: no spots, no traffic.

## Where to find it

- **Set it up:** Settings → **Jukebox**.
- **Open the display:** ☰ menu (top right) → **Jukebox**.
- **Put it on the board:** the source dropdown at the top of a source channel (D, E, F or S1–S5) → **Jukebox**.
- **Phone and lobby addresses:** Settings → **Links**, under the jukebox entries (**Jukebox request page**, **Lobby screen**).

**Plan:** Live 128, Live 320 or Network.

## 1. Choose the music the public can pick

1. Open **Settings → Jukebox**.
2. You see every category on this station, with the number of songs in each that the jukebox can play.
3. Tick the categories you are happy for the public to choose from. Each tick saves straight away. There is no Save button for the ticks. A line under the list confirms **Saved.**
4. Under the list, **Pool:** shows the real total, for example "Pool: 1,284 songs across 4 categories."

- A category showing **no playable songs** has nothing the jukebox can use. Ticking it adds nothing.
- Only music counts. Songs with no file on this computer are left out on purpose, so a public pick can never produce dead air.
- If the station has no categories yet, the screen tells you to create them first and come back.

## 2. Name your request page (the QR code)

1. In **Settings → Jukebox**, find **Request link (QR)**.
2. Type a short name, for example `party`. Use lowercase letters, numbers and dashes, and start with a letter or number.
3. Press **Save**. The line below says **Request link saved.**
4. The Jukebox window turns the name into an address (for example `listen.ether-technologies.com/jukebox/party`) and shows it as a large QR code under the queue, with the words **Request from your phone**.

- Leave the box empty and the Jukebox shows no code. It says **No request link set yet** instead.
- Type a name, not a web address. The box also accepts a full `https://` address and puts it in the QR code as typed, but the phone request page described in section 6 only works with a short name.

## 3. Set the request limits

1. In **Settings → Jukebox**, find **Request limits**.
2. **Max waiting** — how many requests may wait at once. Default 12. Allowed 1 to 500.
3. **Repeat after … minutes** — how long before the same song can be requested again. Default 60. Allowed 0 to 1440. Set **0** to allow a song straight back.
4. Press **Save**. The line below says **Request limits saved.**

These rules apply to requests typed at the Jukebox window and to requests from phones alike. The repeat window counts any play of the song on this computer, including a play from your normal log, not only jukebox plays.

## 4. Put the jukebox on the board

1. On the board, go to a source channel: D, E, F or S1–S5.
2. Open the source dropdown at the top of the channel and choose **Jukebox**.
3. Make sure the channel is **ON** and bring the fader up when you want it heard.

- Decks A, B and C cannot be a jukebox. Automation runs those decks, so the jukebox never shares a deck with your log.
- Use one jukebox channel. If more than one channel is set to Jukebox, the jukebox plays on the first one (in channel order).
- You can change the patch while the Jukebox window is open. The window notices within a few seconds.

## 5. Open and run the display

1. Click the **☰ menu** at the top right → **Jukebox**.
2. It opens in its own window, fullscreen. Drag it to the screen the public will see.

What the public sees:

- **NOW PLAYING** across the top: the cover, then the requester's name, the song title and the artist. When nothing is playing it says **Nothing playing — pick a song below.**
- **Search by song or artist…** under it. The wall narrows as you type. There is no button to press.
- **The wall of covers.** Scroll to see more; more covers load as you go. Covers come from the artwork inside your music files and from the iTunes catalogue. A cover with no artwork yet shows a coloured tile with the song title.
- **THE QUEUE** on the right, with the number of requests waiting. **● UP NEXT** is at the top: the next waiting request, or, if nobody is waiting and AUTO is on, the song the jukebox has already chosen. Behind it, **AFTER THAT** lists everyone else, numbered #2, #3, #4… in the order they asked.
- Every entry shows three lines: the person's name, the song title, the artist. When the jukebox picked the song itself, the name reads **Unknown**.
- The QR code at the bottom right.

To request a song at the screen:

1. Tap a cover.
2. Type a name in **Your name** (up to 40 characters).
3. Press **PLAY NEXT** (or press Enter). Press **Cancel** to go back.
4. A message thanks the person and says the song is in the queue or up next.

How requests play:

- Requests play in the order they arrived. A request never cuts a song that is already playing; it starts when the current song ends.
- **One song at a time per person.** Someone with a request still waiting is told *You already have "…" waiting. You can pick another once it has played.*
- A song that is already on the list cannot be added again: *"…" is already on the list.*
- A song that played recently is refused with roughly how many minutes to wait.
- When the queue is full: *The queue is full right now — 12 songs are already waiting. Try again shortly.*
- If a requested song cannot be played, the jukebox says *Couldn't play "…" — skipping to the next request.* and moves on.

## The OPERATOR button

The small **OPERATOR** button at the right of the NOW PLAYING strip holds the controls the public should not see. Click it to open or close them.

- It reads **ON AIR** with a flashing red dot when the jukebox deck is playing, its channel is ON and its fader is up. All three are needed.
- Its border and dot turn amber when something needs attention: not routed to a channel, channel OFF, or fader down.
- Inside, a line tells you the routing state, for example *Routed to Deck D · channel ON · AUTO on…*, or what is wrong: not routed, channel OFF, or fader down. Requests keep being collected in every case.
- **AUTO ON / AUTO OFF** is the jukebox's own AUTO. It has nothing to do with your station's AUTO/MANUAL. It starts **OFF** each time the window opens.
  - **AUTO ON** — between requests the jukebox keeps music going, shuffled from the categories you ticked.
  - **AUTO OFF** — only requested songs play. When the queue is empty the jukebox is silent, on purpose.
- Next to AUTO: the number of songs in the pool and the names of the ticked categories.

## 6. Requests from phones

When a short name is saved in **Request link (QR)** and categories are ticked, Ether publishes the pool to your request page. A guest who scans the QR code:

1. Sees **REQUEST A SONG** and your station name.
2. Types a name in **YOUR NAME**.
3. Searches in **PICK A SONG** and taps a song (a tick appears).
4. Presses the button at the bottom, which reads **Request "…"**.
5. Sees *You're in the queue!* and *Watch the screen — your name will appear in the queue.* **Request another** starts again.

- Phone requests follow the same rules as the screen (one at a time, max waiting, repeat window). The phone cannot always be told when a rule refuses a request, which is why the page says "watch the screen."
- If this computer is offline, the page says the request is saved and will join the queue when the booth is back.
- Changes to the ticked categories reach the phone page within about a minute.
- If the name does not match a published jukebox, the phone shows **This jukebox isn't set up yet**.

## 7. The lobby screen (optional)

A second, read-only screen for a room, showing **NOW PLAYING**, **COMING UP** and the QR code. Find its address in **Settings → Links** → **Lobby screen** (it is your request address with `/lobby` on the end) and open it in a browser on any screen. It updates only while the Jukebox window is open on this computer.

## Moving and closing the window

- **Escape** leaves fullscreen so you get the normal window bar back. **F11** goes back to fullscreen. Escape never stops audio and never closes the window.
- Close the window like any window. Closing it does not stop your station.

## Good to know

- **The jukebox plays to one station: the active one.** With no active station it shows **No station selected** and does nothing. Sign in, pick a station in the main window, then reopen the Jukebox.
- **The jukebox isn't set up yet** means no categories are ticked. Go to Settings → Jukebox.
- **No songs available to pick** with categories ticked means the ticked categories have no songs whose files are on this computer.
- **The song list couldn't be loaded** is a fault, not an empty library. Check the log.
- Ticks you change in Settings show on an open Jukebox window within a few seconds; you do not need to reopen it.
- Jukebox songs are written to Play History like any other play, marked as jukebox plays.
- There is no priority and no paying to skip the line. Requests are first come, first served.

# 18. Screens

## What it's for

A screen is a window you lay out yourself from Ether's own panels — **Carts**, **Program Log** and **Song
Library** — side by side, stacked, or as tabs, at the sizes you want. You save it under a name and open it again
whenever you need it. Typical screens: two cart walls and the log for a live show; the library and the log for
building the day; carts and the library on a second monitor.

The panels are the real ones, not copies. A cart fired from your screen plays exactly as from the Carts window, and
an edit in your screen's Program Log is the same log as everywhere else.

## Where to find it

**☰ menu → Screens.** It opens a new, empty screen in its own window.

**Plan:** Local (every plan).

---

## A screen belongs to one station

A screen works on the station that was active when you opened it. The station's name is shown in the screen's top
bar. Switching station in the main window does not change an open screen.

---

## Build a screen

1. **☰ menu → Screens.** A new window opens. It is in **EDIT**, and its middle says **An empty screen**.
2. **Add panels.** Press **+ Carts**, **+ Program Log** or **+ Song Library** in the top bar. Each new panel opens
   beside the last one. You can add the same panel more than once (two cart walls, two logs).
3. **Arrange them:**
   - Drag a panel's tab onto another panel to stack them as tabs.
   - Drag a tab to the edge of a panel to split the space.
   - Drag the line between two panels to resize them.
   - Press **×** on a tab to remove that panel.
4. **Save.** Press **SAVE** and type a name. After that, **SAVE** updates the same screen. The top bar shows the
   name (or **Unsaved screen** until you save), and a short message confirms *Saved "name"*.
5. **Lock it.** Press **EDIT**. It changes to **LIVE**: panels can no longer be dragged, resized or closed, and
   every panel keeps working. Press **LIVE** to go back to EDIT.

---

## Open a saved screen

1. In any screen window, press **OPEN…**.
2. A list shows the screens saved for this station (**Screens on *station***). Pick one.
3. It opens in its own window, in **LIVE**, exactly as you saved it.

**NEW SCREEN** opens another empty screen window.

Each saved screen remembers where its window was on this computer.

---

## Good to know

- Only **Carts**, **Program Log** and **Song Library** can be placed on a screen.
- Cart keys belong to the cart wall you last clicked (see chapter 9).
- If a screen will not open and says *That screen is not saved on this station.*, it belongs to another station:
  switch to that station and use **OPEN…** there.
- A saved screen cannot yet be renamed or deleted from the screen window.
- Screens are saved for the station, not per user profile.
- **Plan:** a saved screen's arrangement goes to your other computers through Multi-computer sync, which needs
  **Live 128, Live 320 or Network**. Without it, screens stay on this computer.

# 19. Remote Access & Website

## What it's for

While Ether is open, it answers other devices **on the same network as this computer** (the same Wi-Fi or office
network). Two things are offered:

- **Phone remote**: a page for a phone or tablet. It shows what is playing and what is up next, with **⏭ Skip**,
  **⏹ Stop all**, **AUTO on** and **AUTO off** for the station you are working on. No app is needed, only the phone's
  browser.
- **Now playing, on your network**: an address that any page on the same network can read to show the current song,
  for example a screen in the lobby or a studio sign.

This works only inside your own network. A phone on mobile data, or a public website on the internet, cannot reach
it. For your public website, use your station's listener page or the embed player (see "Good to know").

## Where to find it

**File → Settings → Broadcast → Remote Access & Website.**

**Plan:** Local (every plan). It runs on this computer alone.

---

## Use the phone remote

1. On the computer, open **File → Settings → Broadcast** and go to **Remote Access & Website**.
2. Under **Phone remote**, read the address, for example `http://192.168.1.20:4242`. Press **Copy** if you want to
   send it to the phone.
3. Make sure the phone is on the same Wi-Fi as this computer.
4. On the phone, open that address in the browser. The page is titled **ETHER REMOTE**.
5. The phone asks you to **Enter the 6-digit code**. Type the code shown next to **Code the phone asks for:** in
   Settings and tap **Connect**. The phone remembers the code.
6. The phone now shows the station's name, **Now playing** (or **Not playing**), the title and artist, and the next
   five items under **Up next**. It updates every few seconds.
7. Tap **⏭ Skip**, **⏹ Stop all**, **AUTO on** or **AUTO off**. The phone says **Done.**, or **Not done:** and the
   reason.

The commands always go to the station you are working on in Ether's main window.

### Change the code

1. In Settings, press **New code** next to the code.
2. A new 6-digit code appears. Every phone has to enter the new code. A phone with the old code is sent back to the
   code box and told *"That code is not right."*

## Show what's playing on a page on your network

1. In **Remote Access & Website**, under **Now playing, on your network**, copy the address. It is the phone remote's
   address followed by `/now-playing.json`.
2. Give it to whoever builds the page or screen. The page can read it every few seconds.

It returns the station's name, the song title, the artist, whether it is playing, and when the song started. No code
is needed to read it.

---

## Good to know

- **Same network only.** The address shown is this computer's address on your local network. Phones and pages on
  other networks cannot reach it.
- **For a public website, use your listener page or the embed player.** See **Settings → Broadcast → Your listener
  page** and **Settings → Links** (chapter 22).
- **The first time, Windows may ask whether to allow Ether on your network.** Allow it for private networks, or
  phones cannot reach it. Settings says *"Ether is answering on port 4242"* when Ether is ready.
- **If another program already uses port 4242**, Settings shows *"Not available: …"* in red, and the remote cannot be
  used.
- **After 5 wrong codes within a minute**, that phone is refused until the minute is up.
- **The commands run in Ether's main window.** If the main window is not open, the phone says so.
- The phone remote works with no internet connection, as long as the phone and this computer share the network.

# 20. Logs and reports

## What it's for

The **Play Log** is the permanent record of what actually aired on this station. From it you can look up any play, see which computer aired it, check spots against what was scheduled, and export the files that royalty societies and advertisers ask for: a standard as-run CSV, BMI and ASCAP files, a printable PDF, a traffic affidavit and an as-run reconciliation. **Listener Analytics** turns the same record into charts: plays per day and per hour, top songs, top artists and time on air by category.

The Play Log is the record. It is not the plan. The plan is the Program Log (see chapter 7).

## Where to find it

- **Play Log:** menu **Schedule → Play Log**, or the ☰ menu (top right) → **Play Log**. It opens in its own window.
- **Listener Analytics:** menu **Tools → Listener Analytics**.

**Plan:** Local (every plan). Everything in this chapter reads this computer's own records.

## 1. Look up what aired

1. Open **Schedule → Play Log**. The page title reads **Play Log** with the line *What actually aired. Permanent record.*
2. Pick a period with the buttons at the top: **Today**, **7 Days**, **30 Days** or **All**.
3. For an exact range, fill the two date boxes (from → to). You can also fill the two time boxes to narrow it to part of a day. An empty time box means the whole day. Typing a date or time switches the period to a custom range.
4. Three counts sit above the list: **Songs Played**, **Unique Artists** and **Unique Songs**.
5. Each row shows when it aired, the title, artist, deck, category and the computer it aired on (**Aired on**).

Every play in the period is listed. There is no row limit.

## 2. Narrow the list

These filters work on the **Play Log** view only.

1. **Search title or artist…** — type part of a title or artist name.
2. **All decks** — pick one deck to see only what played on it.
3. **All computers** — pick one computer to see only what it aired. Plays from before computers were recorded show as **Not recorded (before 10-18)**.
4. The element-type filter (music, spots, sweepers and so on) shows a count for each type. Tick the types you want.
5. **Clear** (next to the filters) resets search, deck, computer and type in one go.

The computer filter also applies to the exports below.

## 3. Export for royalties and records

With the **Play Log** view showing, the export buttons are on the right of the top bar. Each one covers the whole period you picked, not just what is on screen.

- **Export CSV** — the as-run file: start time, end time, duration, date, title, artist, deck, category, advertiser, ISCI, cart number, status and **Aired On**.
- **BMI** — a CSV in BMI's layout: Title, Performer, Date Of Use, Time Of Use, Duration.
- **ASCAP** — a CSV in ASCAP's layout: Title, Artist, Date, Start Time, Duration (min), Source (your station name).
- **PDF** — opens a printable **Traffic Log** page (station name, period, which computer, every play) and starts the print dialog. Choose **Save as PDF** in the print dialog to keep a file.

1. Pick the period (and a computer, if you want only one).
2. Press the export you need.
3. Choose where to save the file.

The BMI and ASCAP files leave out the **Aired On** column so they stay in each society's exact format. The computer filter still applies to them. A play whose length was never recorded has a blank duration rather than a made-up one.

## 4. Spots against the schedule (Traffic)

1. At the top of the Play Log, press **Traffic** (next to **Play Log**). The title changes to **Traffic**: *Spots scheduled against spots aired.*
2. Four counts sit at the top: **Spots Scheduled**, **Aired**, **Missed** and **Aired of Due** (a percentage; green at 95% and up).
3. The table lists each spot: scheduled time, aired time, the difference in seconds, status (**AIRED**, **MISSED** or pending), cart, ISCI, advertiser, title and length. A difference of more than two minutes shows amber.
4. Press **Export Traffic CSV** for the affidavit. It adds the date, agency and spot type columns.

- If no spots were scheduled in the period, the page says so and tells you how to get spots into the log: add spot breaks to a clock and run **Fill Day** in the Program Log.
- If some spots have no advertiser, cart or ISCI on file, an amber line counts them. They still export, with those columns blank. Fill them in under **Spots & Promos** so the affidavit is billable.

## 5. As-Run reconciliation

1. Press **As-Run** in the top bar.
2. A panel titled **As-Run Reconciliation** opens. Every item the log scheduled in the period is matched against whether it aired:
   - **✓ MATCH** — scheduled and aired.
   - **✗ MISSED** — scheduled and did not air.
   - **+ UNSCHED** — aired but was not in the log.
   - **· PENDING** — still to come.
3. Press **Export As-Run CSV** to save it (time, scheduled title and artist, actual title and artist, status, duration, aired on).
4. Press **Close** to hide the panel.

## 6. Clear the play log

The red **Clear** button at the end of the top bar erases this station's entire play log after you confirm (*Clear the entire play log?*). This cannot be undone. Export anything you need first.

## 7. Listener Analytics

1. Open **Tools → Listener Analytics**.
2. Pick a range at the top: **7 days**, **30 days**, **90 days** or **All time**.
3. Use the four tabs:
   - **Overview** — **Total Plays**, **Unique Songs**, **Unique Artists** and **Avg/Day**; a **Daily Play Volume** chart; **Plays by Hour of Day**; **Category Breakdown** (share of plays and hours per category); and **Recent Plays**.
   - **Top Songs** — each song's plays, category, BPM, LUFS and when it last played.
   - **Top Artists** — each artist's plays, how many different songs, and when they last played.
   - **Scheduling** — **Peak Broadcast Hours** (the busiest hours of the day) and **Time on Air by Category**.
4. Press **✕** to close.

## Good to know

- Listener Analytics counts **plays from this computer's play log**. Despite its name, it does not count listeners.
- The Play Log keeps a record per station. Switch station first to see another station's log.
- The totals at the top of Listener Analytics count music only; spots, sweepers and other elements are left out.
- On a computer used only now and then for playout, the log only holds what that computer aired while it was running.

# 21. Backups and keeping on air

## What it's for

This chapter covers the ways Ether protects your station. **Backups** keep copies of your station's setup (library list, clocks, shows, schedule, settings) on this computer and in the cloud, so you can go back to the copy from just before a mistake. **Keep my stuff synced** keeps your setup and your audio current in the cloud and on every computer signed into your account. **Keep My Station On Air** restarts Ether if it crashes and logs Windows back in after a reboot, so the station returns to air unattended. The **tray icon** keeps Ether playing when its window is closed.

## Where to find it

- **Backups and sync:** **File → Settings → Backup & Restore**.
- **Cloud Log Backup:** **Tools → Cloud Log Backup**.
- **Keep My Station On Air:** **File → Settings → System**.
- **Tray icon:** the Ether icon in the Windows system tray (bottom right of the taskbar).

**Plan:**
- Backups on this computer, Keep My Station On Air and the tray: Local (every plan).
- Cloud backups, **Keep my stuff synced** and **Cloud Log Backup**: Live 128, Live 320 or Network.

## 1. Backups on this computer (and in the cloud)

In **Settings → Backup & Restore**, find **Backups on this computer**. Each computer has its own settings here.

1. **On this computer** — choose how often this computer saves a backup to its own disk: **Off**, every 5, 10, 15 or 30 minutes, every hour, 2, 6 or 12 hours, or **Every day**.
2. **keep the last** — how many of those backups to keep. The line below shows the last backup, when the next one runs, and roughly how much disk space your setting uses.
3. **In the cloud** — the same choice for backups sent to the cloud.
4. **keep** — how many cloud backups of this computer to keep. **0** keeps all of them.
5. Press **Back up now** on either row to make one straight away.

Each change is saved as you make it. The line under the rows says **✓ Saved on this computer.**

Your audio files are not part of these backups. Audio goes to the cloud through **Keep my stuff synced** (section 3).

## 2. Restore a backup

Under **Every backup — pick the one from just before a mistake** you see every backup this computer can use: its own local backups and every cloud backup on your account, from any of your computers. Each row shows the date and time, the computer, where it is kept (**On this computer** or **Cloud**) and its size.

1. Switch **Keep my stuff synced** off first (section 3), so the restore does not spread before you are ready.
2. Find the backup from just before the mistake.
3. Press **Restore** and confirm.
4. When it says it is done, close Ether fully and open it again so everything takes effect.

- A restore replaces this computer's station database. Changes this computer had not yet sent are discarded, and the message tells you how many.
- If you see *sign in to see cloud backups*, only local backups can be listed until you sign in.

### Send a restore to every computer

After a restore, an amber card **This computer was restored from a backup** may appear under the sync switch. It means this computer had already sent changes to your other computers since that backup. If you do nothing, switching sync back on brings those changes back here too.

1. Keep sync switched off.
2. Type your 4-digit admin PIN.
3. Press **Send this restore to every machine** and confirm. The confirmation says how many rows go back to the backup's version and how many are removed everywhere.
4. Switch **Keep my stuff synced** back on.

Other computers' own changes are kept unless they changed one of the same rows. Then the backup's version wins.

## 3. Keep my stuff synced

At the top of **Settings → Backup & Restore** is **Keep my stuff synced**: one sentence that says where you stand, and one switch.

1. Read the sentence. For example: *Everything on this computer is in the cloud — your whole setup and all 1,240 audio files, as of 5 minutes ago.* If something is behind, it says so and offers a button such as **Back up now**, **Finish sending** or **Try again**.
2. Use the switch to turn it on or off **for this computer**. The line under it confirms what happened.
3. Leave it on in normal use.

When it is on, your setup is kept current in both directions on every computer signed into your account. **New audio comes down on its own.** Audio you add on this computer goes up when you press **Send just the audio** (below) or the card's **Back up now** / **Finish sending** button.

### Advanced

Open **Advanced** under the switch for the manual controls. You don't need them in normal use.

- **Going to the cloud right now** — **ON** or **OFF**, as reported by the backup itself. If the switch says off, this must say **OFF**.
- **Audio transfer** — how often new audio is checked for coming down, when it last checked, and what it pulled.
- **Your audio files** — how many of your audio files are in the cloud and how many are still to send.
- **WHERE YOUR AUDIO LIVES** — your catalogue folder. Press **Change folder** to pick another.
- **Send just the audio** — sends the audio files that are not in the cloud yet. **Stop** cancels it. Tick **Re-send every file, even ones already uploaded** to send everything again.
- **Having trouble? Check connection** — checks that this computer can reach the cloud.
- **Cloud audio cleanup** — **Release cloud audio now** removes from the cloud the audio of songs deleted long enough ago, after checking no live song uses the file and nothing scheduled needs it. It needs your 4-digit admin PIN. It never runs on its own.

### Advanced — sync diagnostics

Lower on the same tab, **Advanced — sync diagnostics** shows this computer, your stations' identities, how many changes are waiting to sync, and the sync engine's status.

- **PREFLIGHT** — refresh the readings.
- **PUSH NOW** — send this computer's waiting changes now.
- **PULL NOW** — fetch changes from your other computers now.
- **CLEAR PENDING…** — throw away this computer's waiting changes and start fresh from now. You must type **CLEAR** to confirm. No songs, stations, clocks, logs or settings are deleted, but it cannot be undone.

Before switching sync on for a second computer, compare the station identities shown here with that computer's. If they do not match, the two installs will not merge cleanly.

## 4. Cloud Log Backup

**Tools → Cloud Log Backup** is a second screen for cloud backups.

1. **Backup to R2 Now** — makes a full backup of this station's setup in the cloud straight away. **Recent Backups** below it lists the last five, with time, size and a check code.
2. **Catalogue → Cloud** — **Back Up Catalogue to Cloud** sends every audio file in your catalogue folder (songs, carts, spots, announcements, sweepers, voice tracks). Files already in the cloud are skipped.
3. **Install Station from Cloud** — on a **new** computer, press **Install Station from Cloud** to pull your whole station down: first the database, then the audio. It replaces the local database. If this computer already has songs of its own, Ether shows the date of the cloud backup and asks again before replacing anything. When it finishes, restart Ether.
4. **Connection** — **Account** shows **Uses this computer's sign-in**, and **Station ID (your email)** fills in by itself. Press **Connect** to list **Saved Backups**.
5. **Create Backup** — **Backup Now** makes the same full backup as step 1.
6. **Saved Backups** — backups kept by older versions of Ether. Press **✕** on a row to delete it.

## 5. Keep My Station On Air

On Windows, Ether can restart itself after a crash and log Windows back in after a reboot.

1. Open **File → Settings → System** and find **Keep My Station On Air**.
2. Turn on **Enable auto-recovery after reboot**.
3. Type the Windows password for the account shown, then press **Enable**.
4. Windows asks for permission once. Allow it.
5. Three status lines show the result: **Automatic logon** (Configured), **Startup task** (Registered) and **Watchdog** (Running).

- **Repair** — re-enter the password and apply the setup again, if a status line is not green.
- **Disable** — removes automatic logon and the startup task and stops the watchdog.
- If Ether keeps crashing and restarting, a red line says **Crash-loop alarm tripped — see Station Health.** See chapter 23.

The password is used once and kept in Windows' own encrypted store. Ether does not save it. **Warning (shown on screen):** anyone who reboots this PC will then land in your logged-in Windows session without a password.

On a Mac this section says *Automatic recovery is available on Windows only.*

## 6. Closing the window and the tray icon

When you close Ether's main window, it asks what you want:

- **Keep Playing in Tray** — the window closes and audio keeps going. Ether stays in the system tray.
- **Stop & Quit Ether** — stops automation and the stream, shuts the audio engine down and exits. It won't restart by itself.
- **Cancel** — nothing changes.

If the tray icon could not be created on this computer, the dialog offers only **Stop & Quit Ether** and **Cancel**, so Ether is never left playing with nothing to click.

**The tray icon:**

- Click it to show or hide the Ether window.
- Right-click it for the menu:
  - **Show Ether** — bring the window back.
  - **Stop Keeping On Air…** — stops the on-air watchdog for this session, so Ether won't relaunch if it closes or is force-closed. Useful when you need to shut the computer down fully. Opening Ether again restores it.
  - **Quit Ether** — the same full stop as **Stop & Quit Ether**.

**File → Quit Ether** (Ctrl+Q) does the same full stop.

## Good to know

- Backups cover your station's setup, not your audio files. Audio is protected by sending it to the cloud.
- A restore always needs a restart of Ether to take full effect.
- Restoring and sending a restore expect sync to be **off**. Switch it back on afterwards.
- Closing the window is not quitting. To update Ether or fully stop it, use **Quit Ether**.

### Delete a station

1. Press **Delete** on its row and confirm.
2. If it is the station on air, Ether switches to another station first.

## 5. Settings → Audio: Song Analysis (loudness and BPM)

  2. Type your account email twice (or **RESET** twice, if no email is shown).

# 23. Health Monitor and System Health

## What it's for

The **Health Monitor** is the one screen that answers "is my station OK right now?". It gathers what Ether measures — how much log is left, what the audio is doing, whether rotation hits its targets, whether breaks fire on time, whether the crash watchdog is running — and puts it in one place. Readings are measured, not assumed. If something has not been measured, the panel says so instead of showing green.

**System Health** and **Station Health** are the same screen, reached from different menus. **Station Health** opens it in its own window, for a wall display.

## Where to find it

- ☰ menu (top right) → **Health Monitor**.
- Menu **Tools → System Health**.
- Menu **Tools → Monitors → Station Health** (its own window).
- The two status lights in the bottom bar. Click either one to open the Health Monitor:
  - The **system light** — hover it for *All systems normal*, *Warning*, *System error* or *HA alarm — auto-restart halted*.
  - The **catalogue light** — **CATALOGUE OK** when every audio file resolves on this computer. Hover it for the cause when it is not.

**Plan:** Local (every plan).

## 1. Open it and pick a view

1. Open the Health Monitor. The header shows how long Ether has been running and that the live readings update every second.
2. Press **WALL VIEW** for a fixed, read-only layout that fits any screen without scrolling. It is made for a studio wall. Press **OPS VIEW** to go back to every panel and control.
3. **Station Health** (the small pop-out button in the header) opens the same screen in its own window. Full-screen that window on a wall display.
4. **✕** closes it.

In **OPS VIEW**, the right-hand column is the **live activity** terminal: what Ether is doing, line by line. In **WALL VIEW** it is a thin **LIVE ACTIVITY** rail; click it to expand, and **LIVE ACTIVITY — MINIMISE** to fold it back.

## 2. At a glance

Three cards across the top:

- **Runway** — how many days of log this station has left before it runs out.
- **Rotation health** — your rotation goals against what the clocks actually call.
- **Queue** — how many items wait behind what is on air.

Below them: **Runway trend** (the last 7 days, so you see it falling before it becomes a problem), audio levels, rotation goals per category, and **Live events** — the health ledger, newest first.

## 3. Alerts

Right under the cards is the **Alerts** list: every alert this station raised, newest first. It is never collapsed or filtered. Tick **every station** to see alerts from all your stations.

Alerts include a log row that aired twice, a log row changed after it aired, something that aired but could not be recorded, and a row that aired after it was marked missed.

The list only reports. Nothing in it fixes or silences anything.

## 4. The detail panels

Below the alerts, the panels are yours to arrange: drag a panel by its header to move it, and collapse the ones you don't need today. Ether remembers the order.

- **Audio Processing** — the loudness chain: loudness in and out, the level before and after, the ride gain and the limiter.
- **Mic Inputs** — whether each mic input is working. **Patch…** takes you to the settings.
- **Remote Link** — the state of a remote broadcast link.
- **Account sign-in** — the state of this computer's account sign-in.
- **PFL Output** — whether the headphone (cue) output is really playing. **Change…** takes you to the settings.
- **Spot Schedule — this hour & next** — where your breaks fall. Hollow markers are still to come, solid ones have aired. A break that is going to be late turns amber before it misses.
- **Core Systems** — **Audio Engine**, **Playout mode**, **Database**, **Schema**, **Dead Air Protection** and **Queue**.
- **High Availability** — **Watchdog Process**, **Supervising This App**, **Startup Task**, **Mutual Supervision**, **Crash-Loop Alarm**, **Process Uptime**, **Audio Output**, **Sync Engine**, **Memory (RSS)** and event-loop lag.
- **Recent Events** — the watchdog's log. **Last Error** shows the most recent error, with **Dismiss**.
- **Library & Rotation** — per station: schedule runway, sweepers, whether audio files are on this computer, the rotation pool, items skipped at load, prefetch lag, rotation depth and goals, and the last Generate. When the last Generate had to bend the rules, a **Last Generate** row appears (see "Slots where a rule was broken" below).
- **Sync changes held** — changes the sync is holding back.
- **Spots that did not air** — breaks that were due and did not play. **RELOAD** reads it again; it changes nothing.
- **Playout & Generate** — the log reader and the last log build.
- **DMCA Play Log Export** — exports the play log as a CSV file (see below). It also shows how many entries the play log holds and when the last one played.

## 5. Things you can do from here

### Clear a crash-loop alarm

If Ether crashed and restarted too many times in a row, the watchdog stops restarting it and the **Crash-Loop Alarm** row turns red.

1. Fix or note the cause (see **Recent Events** and the live activity).
2. In **High Availability**, press **CLEAR & RE-SUPERVISE** on the **Crash-Loop Alarm** row. It removes the alarm, stops the halted watchdog and starts one that looks after the running app again.

### Push or pull sync now

On the **Sync Engine** row in **High Availability**, **PUSH** sends this computer's waiting changes now and **PULL** fetches changes from your other computers now. These are manual overrides.

### Fix files outside the catalogue

At the top of **Library & Rotation**, the **Catalogue** box checks that every audio file sits in your catalogue folder. A file stored elsewhere may play here but will not reach your other computers.

1. Press **SCAN CATALOGUE**. Scanning changes nothing. It reports what it found.
2. If items need fixing, a second button names the work, for example **FIX 12 ITEMS**. Press it to move or relink them.
3. The health figures refresh on the next check.

### Slots where a rule was broken

When Generate builds the log and no song can meet a **STRICT** music rule, it breaks that rule only as the last resort and puts in the category's least recently played song. A STRICT **Max songs in a row** that cannot be met is marked the same way. Those slots are marked on the log row (see the Program Log chapter) and counted here.

1. Open **Library & Rotation** and find the station.
2. If the last Generate broke a rule, a **Last Generate** row shows **separation relaxed ×** and the number of slots.
3. The line under it names up to three categories with the most such slots, for example *"Gold ×4 · Recurrents ×2"*.

If a category had no songs at all, the row instead shows how many categories were empty, in red, and names them.

### Export the play log (CSV)

1. In **DMCA Play Log Export**, press **Export Play Log CSV**.
2. A Save window opens. Pick a folder and a file name (it suggests one with today's date) and press **Save**.
3. The button shows **✓ Saved** when the file is written.

- The file has one row per played item, newest first: date, time, title, artist, deck and a timestamp. It holds up to the last 10,000 plays recorded on this computer, from every station on it.
- If you press **Cancel** in the Save window, nothing is written.
- If the file cannot be written, a red line says *"Play log not exported:"* and the reason.

### See which songs can't be played

When **Library & Rotation** shows songs whose audio is not on this computer, press **show unresolvable list** under that station to see them, so you can find and fix each one. **hide unresolvable list** folds it away.

## Good to know

- The same screen opens from all three menus.
- The Health Monitor reports. Apart from the buttons named in this chapter, it changes nothing. **Export Play Log CSV** only writes a file; it does not change the play log.
- A grey reading means "not measured yet", not "fine".

# 24. Keyboard shortcuts, Help and About

## What it's for

This chapter lists the keys that work in Ether and explains the **Help** menu: the shortcut list, the documentation, checking for updates, and the About screen.

## Where to find it

- **The shortcut list:** **Help → Keyboard Shortcuts**, or press **Shift + ?** on the main screen.
- **Help menu:** the menu bar at the top of the window → **Help**.

**Plan:** Local (every plan). Updates are included in every plan.

## Keyboard shortcuts

Ether's shortcuts are **single keys**: press the key on its own. Click somewhere that is **not a text box** first; while you are typing in a box, the keys type.

### On the main screen

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| **A** | Turns AUTO on or off (the same as clicking the AUTO / MANUAL button). |
| **L** | Opens the Library. |
| **S** | Opens Schedule (Clocks). |
| **G** | Opens the Play Log. |
| **Shift + ?** | Shows or hides the shortcut list. |
| **Esc** | Closes the shortcut list and the ☰ menu. Esc never stops audio. |

### On a cart wall

| Keys | What they do |
|---|---|
| **1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8** | Fire carts 1 to 8. |
| **Q W E R T Y U I** | Fire carts 9 to 16. |
| **A S D F G H J K** | Fire carts 17 to 24. |

Only carts that have audio on them fire. Carts after the 24th are click-only. If more than one cart wall is showing in the same window, the keys belong to the wall you clicked last.

**While a cart wall is open, these keys fire carts only.** That includes the cart wall under the board (the **CARTS** tab) and a deck slot set to a cart wall. **A**, **S** and **G** then fire carts 17, 18 and 21; they do not switch AUTO, open Clocks or open the Play Log.

### In the Cue Editor

The letter keys work when nothing in the editor has the focus, for example right after it opens. After you click the waveform, a button or a box, they do nothing until you click an empty part of the editor outside the waveform. **Space** also works after you click the waveform.

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Space** | Plays or pauses. |
| **I** | Jumps to **CUE IN**. |
| **N** | Jumps to **INTRO END**. |
| **O** | Jumps to **OUTRO START**. |
| **U** | Jumps to **CUE OUT**. |

The four letters are shown on the cue-point strip. A jump is the same as clicking that point: if the song was playing, it keeps playing from there.

### On the profile screen

While entering a PIN: the number keys type the PIN, **Backspace** deletes a digit, and **Esc** goes back to the list of profiles.

### On a fader

Click a fader first. The **arrow keys** step it up or down, and **Enter** lets you type a level.

### Menu shortcuts

| Keys | What they do |
|---|---|
| **Ctrl + N** (⌘ + N on Mac) | **File → New Session**: returns to the main screen. |
| **Ctrl + Q** (⌘ + Q) | **File → Quit Ether**: stops the audio engine and closes Ether completely. |
| **Ctrl + R** (⌘ + R) | **View → Reload**: reloads the window. You stay signed in while Ether runs; the window comes straight back without the sign-in screen. |

2. Press **Copy** next to the box you want.
3. Paste the code into your website.
4. Optional: add `&theme=light` for a light background, or `&accent=%23ff5500` (any color code) to change the
   accent color. For **Recently Played**, `&limit=` sets how many songs show (1 to 50, 8 if you leave it out).

The boxes refresh on their own. If the station's page is turned off, the box says **Station unavailable**.

## Good to know

- **Changes can take up to a minute to show** on the page after you save them.
- **The stream must be set.** Without a stream address the page shows **Stream not set up yet** and cannot play.
- **Now-playing comes from the station's computer.** The song, art and queue show what Ether on the studio
  computer reports. If that computer is off, the page shows **Off air**.
- **Cover art.** The page uses the art from the song file when there is one, otherwise it looks the song up.
  Station imaging and commercials never get a looked-up cover; they show your logo instead.
- **Brand.** The browser tab and the phone's lock screen show your organization's name followed by "Radio", taken
  from your hub name, on every station page.
- **Listener numbers are private.** Each listener is counted without names or addresses. You see the counts in
  the web dashboard's **Listeners** tab. Nothing is shown to the public.
- **Some stations have their own page design.** A few stations have a custom-designed page in place of the
  standard one (currently the stations at `/magical-forest` and `/halloween`). These are made by Ether
  Technologies for that station; there is no setting to pick a design.
- **Jukebox pages** (`/jukebox/…`) live on the same site. See chapter 17.
